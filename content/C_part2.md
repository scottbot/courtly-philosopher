::: seg id=C-038b pdf=38-39 page=32-33 sig=B5 kind=prose squares=39
ES: MAS Adelante a trenta[sic] y nueue casas esta la de la falsa amistad, que es de la que vsan los falsos amigos que como Raposa, que se haze muerta por cojer los paxaros, que la vienen a picar ellos se hazen muy humildes y faciles al trato, prometiendo impossibles, que lo son de cumplir, hasta cojer lo que pueden: a lo qual es menester aunque se entienda siruiendo al tiempo, dissimular con ellos, y para no hazer de amigos enemigos, ni dar lugar a que del todo se declaren por tales mostrando satisfacion de su voluntad, hazer lo que dize su letra.
EN: Further on, at thirty-nine houses, is the house of False Friendship: the friendship that false friends practice. Like the Fox, who plays dead to catch the birds that come to peck at her, they make themselves very humble and easy to deal with, promising impossibilities (impossible, that is, to fulfill) until they have caught what they can. Against this one must, even while seeing through it, bend to the times and dissemble with them; and, so as not to make enemies of friends, nor give them occasion to declare themselves such outright, one must show oneself satisfied with their goodwill and do what its verse says.
NOTE: "prometiendo impossibles, que lo son de cumplir": Barros's small joke, "promising impossibles, which are so [impossible] to fulfill", is kept by the parenthesis. "siruiendo al tiempo": literally "serving the time", i.e. temporizing, accommodating oneself to circumstances. The Fox that feigns death to catch birds is the bestiary fox; Barros makes her feminine ("Raposa"). "trenta" (for "treinta") is as printed.
:::

::: seg id=C-039b pdf=39-40 page=33-34 sig=B5 kind=prose squares=7
ES: PAra cuyo remedio, se ma[n]da al negociante que buelua a ser prodigo, con los que tuuo antes por mas sospechosos, que al principio todos lo son, hasta que se topan otros peores: lo qual va sinificado por vn Pelicano, que lo es ta[n]to que dexa de dar sus entrañas (que es la hazienda) a sus hijos, y lo da a vnos gatos mostrando la fuerça que se haze a la razon con el prete[n]der: pues no solo ha de ser el hombre liberal con los amigos, sino prodigo con los enemigos, aunque conozca lo poco que en ello se granjea, pues no dura mas su amistad de lo que dize su letra.
EN: As a remedy for this, the one pressing his case is ordered to go back and be prodigal again with those he formerly held most suspect (for at first they all are, until one runs into worse). This is signified by a Pelican, so prodigal that it stops giving its entrails (which are its estate) to its young, and gives them instead to some cats: showing the violence that petitioning for office does to reason. For a man must be not only liberal with his friends but prodigal with his enemies, even though he knows how little is gained by it; for their friendship lasts no longer than its verse says.
NOTE: "buelua a ser prodigo": "go back to being prodigal" is also, in the game, "go back to the Prodigal" (square 7, to which False Friendship, 39, sends the player in the rules below). The English "go back and be prodigal again" keeps both senses only in part. "que lo es tanto": literally "which is so to such a degree", i.e. so prodigal. The pelican that feeds its young with its own flesh and blood is the familiar emblem of self-sacrificing love; Barros inverts it, making it feed cats instead of its brood. "la fuerça que se haze a la razon": "the force done to reason", i.e. how petitioning makes a person act against reason.

ES: TRas esta desgracia, sucede otra no menos graue, que es la mudança de ministros puesta a quarenta y tres casas, porque auiendo el prete[n]sor granjeado con todas sus fuerças algun personaje, ministro de su negocio, de quien se pensaua valer, se le mudan, o el se muda: con lo qual se halla tan falto de acogida, por ser solo que le es fuerça dezir.
EN: After this misfortune comes another no less grievous: the Change of Ministers, placed at forty-three houses. For when the petitioner has, with all his might, won over some great personage, a minister in charge of his case, on whom he thought he could rely, they move that minister away from him, or the man himself changes; and with that the petitioner finds himself so bereft of welcome, being alone, that he is forced to say:
NOTE: "se le mudan, o el se muda": "they change him on him [i.e. the minister is replaced], or he changes". The second "el" most naturally means the minister (moving post, or changing disposition); it could also be read as the petitioner moving on. "por ser solo": literally "because of being alone/only". Read with the verse that follows ("Quien limita su esperança", "Who narrows all his hope to one"), it most likely means that this minister was the only one the petitioner had cultivated; it may also mean simply "because he is left alone". "acogida": welcome, shelter, reception at court.

::: seg id=C-042b pdf=42-43 page=36-37 sig= kind=prose squares=10
ES: MAndasele que buelua de nueuo a la Adulacion, y haga reuerencia al q[ue] sucediere en aquel lugar: lo qual se figura por vna Serena con vn espejo en la vna mano, y en la otra vn Camaleon, porque el adulador, persuade al que pretende engañar con exemplos de casos viciosos, que han sucedido a personas graues, mostrandoselos como en espejo (y para que los imite, y no los conozca, los cubre co[n] capa de virtud) y tambien se muda de difere[n]tes figuras, lleuando el gusto del engañado, como se muda el Camaleon, del color de lo que esta mas cerca. Y finalmente su canto y platica, es, como el de la Serena, para matar por engañno[sic] al suspenso, que eleuado en su vanidad gusta de su musica, y no se pone algodon en los oydos en tan peligroso passo come[sic] este. Y assi dize su letra.
EN: He is ordered to go back once more to Flattery and bow to whoever succeeds to that place. This is figured by a Siren with a mirror in one hand and a Chameleon in the other. For the flatterer persuades the man he means to deceive with examples of vicious deeds that have occurred among persons of weight, showing them to him as in a mirror (and, so that he will imitate them and not recognize them for what they are, he cloaks them with the cape of virtue). He also changes into different shapes, humoring the taste of the man deceived, as the Chameleon changes to the color of whatever is nearest. And finally his song and his talk are, like the Siren's, meant to kill by deceit the spellbound listener, who, rapt in his own vanity, delights in the music and does not put cotton in his ears in so perilous a pass as this. And so its verse says:
NOTE: "buelua de nueuo a la Adulacion": in the game, the Change of Ministers (43) sends the player back to Flattery (10); in the moral, the petitioner must flatter the new minister. "al q[ue] sucediere en aquel lugar": "whoever succeeds to that post", i.e. the new minister. "casos viciosos, que han sucedido a personas graues": literally "vicious cases that have happened to grave persons"; the sense is examples of vice in people of standing, offered as precedents. "no los conozca": "not recognize them" (as vices). "lleuando el gusto": "going along with the taste", humoring. "al suspenso": the one held in suspense, entranced. "passo": both "pass, danger" and "step"; "come" is as printed (for "como"; possibly the Neapolitan compositor's Italian). Barros's Ulysses stops his ears with cotton, not wax.

::: seg id=C-044c pdf=44-46 page=38-40 sig= kind=prose squares=51
ES: TRas la aduersa fortuna viene la prospera, para mostrar, que no ay ninguno tan falto della, a cuya puerta no llegue, y le ayude, si el se sabe aprouechar del tie[m]po, y por esso se pone la fortuna a cincuenta y vna casas con vna letra en la mano, que dize. Yo trueco, y mudo el co[n]sejo, que es lo mismo que dezir. Yo soy la que dispongo vuestros conse[j]os en las determinaciones, y guio vuestras obras en los efetos, para que hagays, o dexeys de hazer diligencias: de donde se colije que no és la diligencia (como algunos dizen) madre de la buena ventura, sino hija y criada a sus pechos: porq[ue] hablando bien, no ay fortuna sino vna dispusicion de la uoluntad de Dios, vniuersal gouernador de todas nuestras acciones, para que con este conocimiento, toleremos con paciencia nuestros males, que haziendo de la fuerça virtud, los conuertiremos en bienes. Pues como la letra lo muestra.
EN: After adverse fortune comes prosperous fortune, to show that there is no one so lacking in it that it does not come to his door and help him, if he knows how to make good use of the time. And for that reason Fortune is placed at fifty-one houses, with a motto in her hand that says: “I exchange and alter counsel.” Which is as much as to say: “I am she who disposes your counsels in your resolutions, and guides your works to their effects, so that you take, or fail to take, diligent steps.” From which it follows that diligence is not (as some say) the mother of good fortune, but her daughter, nursed at her breast. For, properly speaking, there is no fortune, only a disposition of the will of God, universal governor of all our actions; so that, knowing this, we may bear our ills with patience, and, making a virtue of necessity, we shall turn them into goods. For, as the verse shows:
NOTE: "Yo trueco, y mudo el co[n]sejo": the motto Fortune holds on the board, on a banderole (in Italian there, "cambio et muto il consiglio"); literally "I exchange and alter counsel". "hazer diligencias": to take the steps, make the efforts, that pressing a petition requires; the word also names the square of Diligence (20), to which the player is later sent. "no es la diligencia… madre de la buena ventura": Barros is contradicting a saying, that diligence is the mother of good fortune, as his "como algunos dizen" ("as some say") signals. "és": the print has a mark over the e, apparently an acute accent (it may be a speck of ink); kept as printed. "haziendo de la fuerça virtud": the Spanish form of "to make a virtue of necessity" (fuerça = constraint, compulsion).

::: seg id=C-047a pdf=47 page=41 sig= kind=prose squares=51
ES: Y Porque la demasiada dicha, muchas vezes no aprouecha, antes destruye y daña al que no la tiene en saberse aprouechar della, se manda al jugador, que llega a la casa de la fortuna, juegue dos uezes, como lo haze el que es dichoso, para que se vea si lo es, estando el juego de manera, que co[n] dos suertes se gana la polla, y con las demas se pierde, o se dilata.
EN: And because too much luck often does a man no good, but rather destroys and harms the one who has not the luck to know how to profit by it, the player who reaches the House of Fortune is ordered to play twice, as the lucky man does, so that it may be seen whether he is lucky; for the game is so arranged that with two throws the pot is won, and with the rest it is lost or put off.
NOTE: "al que no la tiene en saberse aprouechar della": literally "the one who does not have it [luck] in knowing how to profit by it"; the play on "dicha" (luck) is Barros's. "juegue dos uezes": "play twice"; see the note on the rules (C-066b). "con dos suertes se gana la polla": "suerte" is both "a throw (a total on the dice)" and "luck". Two readings: (a) "with two throws", i.e. over two throws the player can win the pot; (b) "with two [particular] throws", i.e. only two totals win from here. Reading (b) fits the board's arithmetic: from 51 the palm (63) is 12 away, so a throw of 12 lands on it directly, and a throw of 6 lands on the oxen at 57 and, by the rule of the oxen, goes on another 6 to 63. With two dice, 6 and 12 are then the only totals that win from 51 on one throw; 4 (to "I Thought…", 55, sent back to 20) and 9 (to Poverty, 60, sent back to 53) are losses, and the rest merely delay. This is my inference from the board and the rules, not something the text states. "se pierde, o se dilata": the subject is unexpressed (the pot, or the game): "is lost or deferred".
:::

::: seg id=C-047b pdf=47-49 page=41-43 sig= kind=prose squares=51,55
ES: Tambien se deue notar, que aunque tiene mano la fortuna en la elecion de las diligencias, no se han de fiar todas della, ni es causa bastante para que el prete[n]sor se descuyde en hazer lo que pudiere, que son medios ordenados para este fin sin poderle nosotros juzgar. y la tibieza co[n]fiada para el dexarlo todo, es escudo de holgazanes, y mas de los que son tales que no haziendo ellos diligencia en sus negocios piensan que otros las haran. Por esto, y porque es muy de dichosos el descuidarse se pinta a cincue[n]ta y cinco casas la del penseque, figurado por vn asno echado, por la semejanca q[ue] con el tiene, el que dize, Quien pensara, y no lo preuiene: de los quales es cierto lo que su letra dize.
EN: It should also be noted that, although Fortune has a hand in the choice of our diligent steps, they are not all to be left to her; nor is this sufficient cause for the petitioner to be careless in doing what he can, for these are means ordained to this end, though we cannot judge it. And complacent lukewarmness, which leaves everything alone, is the shield of idlers, and still more of those who are such that, taking no pains in their own affairs, they think others will take them. For this reason, and because it is very much the way of the lucky to grow careless, the house of “I Thought…” is painted at fifty-five houses, figured by an ass lying down, because of the likeness it bears to the man who says “Who would have thought it?” and does not provide against it. Of such men, what its verse says is certain.
NOTE: "tiene mano": "has a hand in" (mano is also "a round" of play). "sin poderle nosotros juzgar": "without our being able to judge it"; the singular "le" most probably refers to "este fin" (the end to which Providence orders our means), possibly to the ordering itself. "penseque": the noun made of the excuse "pensé que…", "I thought that…"; the square is named "Pensar che" on the Italian side of the board. "Quien pensara": "who would have thought it?", the careless person's excuse after the event; "preuiene" = foresees and provides against. "escudo de holgazanes": the shield, i.e. the excuse, of idlers.
:::

::: seg id=C-050b pdf=50-52 page=44-46 sig= kind=prose squares=60
ES: Y Con el tiempo, engaño y dilaciones, viene el pretensor a la pobreza, que esta figurada a sesenta casas, por vna tierra tan seca, que no ay hoja, ni fruta en los arboles, para mostrar lo que va a dezir de la Primauera de la abundancia, a l' Inuierno de la necessidad: especial en el efeto que haze en el valedor, conocer de su prete[n]sor que ha venido a pobre: por que en viendo que lo es, luego se le seca de palabras, con ser hoja que lleua el viento, y mucho mas de obras, fruto de la obligacio[n] que nos tenemos vnos a otros. Lo qual era muy al reues en el tie[m]po que el tuuo hoja, y fruto de gusto, para ganar voluntades; que como le ha faltado el riego todas se le ha[n] secado: a cuya causa dize su letra.
EN: And with time, deceit and delays, the petitioner comes to Poverty, which is figured at sixty houses by a land so dry that there is neither leaf nor fruit on the trees, to show how great a difference there is between the Spring of abundance and the Winter of need; especially in the effect it has on the patron to learn that his petitioner has become poor. For as soon as he sees that he is, he at once dries up toward him in words (though words are leaves the wind carries off) and much more in deeds, the fruit of the obligation we owe one another. It was quite the reverse in the time when the petitioner had leaf, and fruit to please, with which to win goodwill; but since the watering has failed him, all those goodwills have withered on him. For which reason its verse says:
NOTE: "lo que va a dezir de… a…": an idiom, "what difference there is from… to…". "se le seca de palabras, con ser hoja que lleua el viento": the patron withholds even words, which cost nothing ("leaves the wind carries off"). "el tuuo": "he had"; most naturally the petitioner, whose money (the "watering") kept favor green. "todas se le ha[n] secado": the feminine plural most likely refers to "voluntades" (goodwills); it could also be the leaves and fruit taken together.

ES: MAndanle dar limosna con que se vaya a buscar otra suerte de negociar, y va a los dados cincuenta, y tres: donde esta vna suerte de onze que suele ser buena y agradable para el que juega, pero como para el pobre ninguna lo es aunque el la escoja, dize su letra.
EN: They order that he be given alms, with which he may go off and seek another way of pressing his case; and he goes to the Dice at fifty-three, where there is a throw of eleven, which is usually good and pleasing for the one who plays. But since for the poor man no throw is, even if he chooses it himself, its verse says:
NOTE: "MAndanle dar limosna" is grammatically ambiguous: "they order him to give alms" or "they order alms to be given him". The rules (C-066b, "danle limosna", "they give him alms") settle it for 1588: the player receives. The 1587 Madrid text, in Ciompi and Seville's modernized transcription, is more explicit ("danle por limosna un tanto cada uno": each player gives that player one stake). "otra suerte de negociar": "another way (suerte) of pressing his case", with the pun on "suerte" (throw of the dice) that leads to the Dice square. "vna suerte de onze": a throw totalling eleven, presumably shown on the dice painted in the square. "aunque el la escoja": "even if he chooses it"; perhaps an allusion to dice games in which the caster names a winning number, perhaps simply "even if it were the one he would pick".

::: seg id=C-053b pdf=53-54 page=47-48 sig= kind=prose squares=63
ES: POR fin destos trabajos, se pone en la casa de la vitoria vna palma, con que fueron antiguame[n]te coronados los que tuuieron valor de sufrimiento en las aduersidades, y ventura en los sucessos para tanta dificultad como tiene el vencer, en cuyo tronco esta vna letra, que dize, Ni lo mucho, ni lo poco. Porque no se han de echar mas, ni menos puntos de los justos para llegar a ella, ni hazer mas, ni menos diligencias de las necessarias, para conseguir lo que se pretende: y por ella ta[m]bien se muestra la templança que el hombre deue tener, no se ensoberueciendo en la prosperidad de la gana[n]cia, ni se acouardando en la aduersidad de la perdida, guardando en todo el medio, que es el niuel de las cosas, y quien les da perfecion: mas como ninguna de las que se alcançan es segura, especial dignidades, dize su letra.
EN: As the end of these labors, a palm is set in the House of Victory: the palm with which, in ancient times, those were crowned who had the valor to endure adversity, and good fortune in the outcome, for all the difficulty there is in winning. On its trunk is an inscription that says: “Neither too much nor too little.” For one must throw neither more nor fewer points than exactly suffice to reach it, nor take more or fewer steps than are needed to obtain what one seeks. And by it is also shown the temperance a man ought to have: not growing proud in the prosperity of gain, nor losing heart in the adversity of loss, keeping in everything to the mean, which is the level of things and what gives them their perfection. But since none of the things attained is secure (dignities above all), its verse says:
NOTE: "trabajos": "labors" and "hardships" at once. "valor de sufrimiento": "the valor of endurance"; "sufrimiento" (suffering, patient endurance) names the Sea at the center of the board (C-056b). "sucessos": outcomes, events. "puntos de los justos": the exact count; the rules (C-066b) make the exact throw a rule of play. "niuel": the mason's level, the standard by which things are made true. The 1588 book never gives the palm's square a number; on the board it is 63.

::: seg id=C-066b pdf=66-69 page=60-63 sig= kind=rules squares=4,12,17,23,30,34,41,48,57,15,26,32,36,28,39,7,43,10,46,51,55,20,60,53,63
ES: EL Que lança el dado, se señala a tantas casas, como puntos echo, y si dos dan en vna casa, se queda el segundo, y el primero toma la que el otro dexo: y si da en los bueyes, passa otras tantas casas adelante, como pu[n]tos echo, para llegar alli. Llegado al passo de la espera[n]ça, que es a quinze casas passa a la del priuado, q[ue] es a veyntiseys, pagando vn tanto por cada vna, y si da en nel[sic] pozo del oluido que es a treynta y dos paga vn tanto a cada vno de los [j]ugadores, y dos en la polla para sogas, auiendo estado sin [j]ugar vna mano: y si da en el Que dira[n], que es a treynta y seys buelue a los dados uey[illeg. ~1 letter]ti[o?]cho y en la falsa amistad, que es a treynta y nueue, buelue al prodigo, q[ue] es a siete casas: y en la mudança de ministros, q[ue] es a quarenta y tres, buelue a la Adulacion a diez: y de la muerte del valedor, que esta a quarenta y seys, buelue a come[n]çar el juego de nueuo, y en la casa de la fortuna que es a cincuenta y vna, juega dos vezes: y del Penseque, q[ue] es a cincuenta y cinco, buelue a la Diligencia, que esta a veynte: y de la Pobreza, que esta a sese[n]ta, buelue a la [illeg. ~1 letter]uerte cincuenta y tres, y danle limosna, y si echa mas pu[illeg. ~1 letter]tos de los iustos, para llegar a la palma, buelue atras los que sobran, paga[n]do vn tanto por cada vez que boluiere atras de qualquier suerte que sea.
EN: Whoever casts the die sets his marker on the house numbered by the points he threw. And if two land on one house, the second stays there, and the first takes the house the other left. And if he lands on the oxen, he passes on as many houses further as the points he threw to get there. Having reached the Pass of Hope, which is at fifteen houses, he passes on to the house of the Favorite, which is at twenty-six, paying one stake for each one. And if he lands in the Well of Oblivion, which is at thirty-two, he pays one stake to each of the players, and two into the pot for ropes, having stayed one round without playing. And if he lands on “What Will They Say?”, which is at thirty-six, he goes back to the Dice at twenty-eight; and on False Friendship, which is at thirty-nine, he goes back to the Prodigal, which is at seven houses; and on the Change of Ministers, which is at forty-three, he goes back to Flattery, at ten; and from the Death of the Patron, which is at forty-six, he goes back to begin the game anew; and in the House of Fortune, which is at fifty-one, he plays twice; and from “I Thought…”, which is at fifty-five, he goes back to Diligence, which is at twenty; and from Poverty, which is at sixty, he goes back to the Dice at fifty-three, and they give him alms. NOTE: (1) "el dado": "the die", singular, but the text elsewhere presupposes two dice: a "throw of eleven" at 53 (C-052b) and, before this part, a losing throw at 28. The Madrid 1587 "Declaración" specifies two dice (or "seis arenillas", six pieces marked on one side only); 1588 does not say how many are used. "el dado" may be generic ("the dice"). (2) "se señala a tantas casas, como puntos echo": "echo" = "echó", "he threw". "se señala": sets one's marker (señal); the 1588 text never describes the counters or the opening stakes, which 1587 explains (each player puts "un tanto" of an agreed value into the pot, "la polla", and uses their own object as a marker). (3) "si dos dan en vna casa, se queda el segundo, y el primero toma la que el otro dexo": the newcomer stays, and the player who was there moves to the newcomer's former square: the two exchange places. The 1587 clause for the start of the game (the displaced first player is left without a square, since the newcomer had none) is absent from 1588. 1588 does not say here whether the displaced player pays; the closing clause ("paying one stake for each time he goes back, of whatever kind") may be meant to cover it (in 1587's worked example the displaced player pays one stake "porque volvió atrás", because he went back). (4) "si da en los bueyes": the oxen stand in the squares of Labor (on the 1588 board, 4, 12, 17, 23, 30, 34, 41, 48 and 57; the text gives no numbers). "passa otras tantas casas adelante, como pu[n]tos echo, para llegar alli": the throw that brought the player there is counted again, so that its value is doubled. The rule is not said to cost anything. (5) "pagando vn tanto por cada vna" (15 → 26) is ambiguous: (a) one stake for each of the two squares, Hope and the Favorite, two stakes in all; (b) one stake for each square passed over between 15 and 26. Reading (a) is supported by the 1587 text ("por cada una de las dos", "for each of the two") and by its worked example (the player pays one stake for the hope offered and another for the favor expected at 26), and by the board, which engraves "Paga" beside both 15 ("Al priuato n.º 26 Paga") and 26 ("Paga"). Where the stakes go is not stated; presumably the pot. "vn tanto": one stake, a counter of the value agreed at the start. (6) Well of Oblivion (32): three things, as the Spanish lists them: one stake to each other player; two stakes into the pot "para sogas", "for ropes" (to haul the player out: the moral text, in the section on the Well, calls them "sogas de liberalidad", "ropes of liberality"); and "auiendo estado sin jugar vna mano", literally "having stayed one round without playing". The participle leaves the order unclear (pay after missing the turn, or pay and miss it); in either case the player misses one round. The board label reads "Para sogas 1 y 2 estese", "For ropes, 1 and 2, and let him stay". (7) "buelue a los dados veyntiocho": the numeral is damaged in the 1588 copy but evidently "veyntiocho" (28). "y en la falsa amistad… y en la mudança de ministros": "and on", where the parallel clauses have "de" ("from"); the sense is the same (landing there sends the player back). Each of these backward moves is marked "Paga" on the board (36, 39, 43, 46, 55), which supports reading the closing clause as general (see 11). (8) "juega dos vezes" (51): "he plays twice". The text does not say whether the player throws again at once (an extra throw on reaching 51), throws twice on their next turn, or adds two throws together; the board says only "Giuoca 2 uolte". For the moral text's "con dos suertes se gana la polla" see the note to C-047a. (9) "buelue a la [s]uerte cincuenta y tres": the first letter is blotted; "suerte" (= "the Dice", as the square of 53 is also called; the board names it "Sorte") is certain from the sense and from the moral text ("va a los dados cincuenta, y tres"). (10) "y danle limosna": "and they give him alms": the verb is plural and impersonal, and neither the givers nor the amount is stated. The 1587 text reads "y danle por limosna un tanto cada uno" (each player gives that player one stake), and adds that the player who lands on Poverty "not only does not pay, but everyone gives him one stake each": that is, the backward move from 60 is exempt from the payment in the final clause. 1588 drops both the amount and the exemption, so read literally 1588 would have the player pay a stake for going back and receive alms of unstated value. The board label reads "Limosina et alla sorte 53", "Alms, and to the Dice 53", and, unlike the labels of the other squares that send the player back, has no "Paga". (11) "pagando vn tanto por cada vez que boluiere atras de qualquier suerte que sea": "de qualquier suerte que sea" = "of whatever kind it may be", with the pun on "suerte" (throw): "from whatever throw it may be". Two scopes are possible: (a) it closes the overshoot rule only (every time the player overshoots the palm and has to count back, they pay one stake, whatever the throw); (b) it is a general rule: every backward move of any kind (36, 39, 43, 46, 55, 60, being displaced) costs one stake. Reading (b) is supported by the 1587 exception for Poverty, which only makes sense if backward moves are otherwise paid for, and by the board's "Paga" on the squares that send the player back. The English keeps the ambiguity. (12) "para llegar a la palma": the palm is not given a number anywhere in the 1588 book (it is 63 on the board). Nor does the 1588 text say how the game is won: 1587's closing rule (whoever reaches 63 with the exact count takes everything in the pot) is absent. The only 1588 statement about winning the pot is in the moral text on Fortune ("con dos suertes se gana la polla", C-047a); the exact-count rule here and the palm's motto "Neither too much nor too little" (C-053b) imply the rest. Quotations of the Madrid 1587 text in these notes are from the modernized transcription by Luigi Ciompi and Adrian Seville (giochidelloca.it), not from the 1587 print.

## Translator's queries

1. **C-044a, "le uee del hombre".** The construction does not parse as printed (1587 modern text "le ve"). I translate the evident sense ("he finds himself bereft of the man who favored him most") and note possible readings. A check of the 1587 princeps image, if available, would help.
2. **C-047a, "con dos suertes se gana la polla".** My note offers the board arithmetic (from 51, only totals of 6, via the oxen at 57, and 12 reach 63 on one throw; 4 and 9 send the player back). This is my inference, labelled as such. Keep it in the reader-facing note, or move it to an apparatus?
3. **Rules (C-066b).** Unresolved in the 1588 text, and so left ambiguous in the English and set out in the notes: whether "el dado" means one die or two; the displaced player's payment; "pagando vn tanto por cada vna" (two stakes vs. one per square passed; I favor two); the order of paying and missing a turn at 32; what "juega dos vezes" means in practice; "danle limosna" with no giver and no amount, and whether the Poverty move back is exempt from payment; the scope of "de qualquier suerte que sea"; and the absence of any 1588 statement of how the game is won. If the web edition's playable rules must decide these, the decisions should be marked as editorial.
4. **C-065a.** The repeated Fortune couplet adds "Que" ("for"). I used the fixed "fort" rendering verbatim, as instructed, and noted the difference. Should a variant be added to the verse table (e.g. "For were she always just the same…")?
5. **ES field.** At the two ink blots in the rules I kept the transcription's convention "[illeg. ~1 letter]" rather than dropping the mark, since dropping it would print "uerte" and "putos". At pdf 61–62 I kept "a- atropellando" (the syllable repeated across the page break). Words split across page breaks ("sospecho-/sos", "cincue[n]-/ta", "preten-/siones", "firme-/za", "vi-/niere", "dere-/cha") are joined, as the transcription joins line-end divisions. A script check confirmed every ES line against the transcription; the only differences are these six joins.
6. **Pagination.** pdf 54 is printed "38" (for 48, corrected by hand in the copy). I give page=47-48 for C-053b.
7. **sig=.** I filled it only where a signature is printed on one of the block's pages (B5, B6, C, C2, C3, C4, C6), written without the space; C-057b spans two signed pages ("C2,C3").
8. **Squares.** For the rules I listed the oxen squares (4, 12, 17, 23, 30, 34, 41, 48, 57) and the palm (63) from the board, although the text numbers neither; the other numbers are the text's own. C-060a (the first Fortune couplet, which closes the corners paragraph) has no square.
9. **Corner figures (C-057b).** Barros names three figures; the note mentions that the board has a fourth device (swan, "Nosce te ipsum") that he does not mention here. Is this better placed in the annotation layer?
10. **External references in notes.** Aulus Gellius, *Noctes Atticae* 3.6 (the palm rising against a weight), the proverb "no se toman truchas a bragas enjutas" (C-055b), and the proverb "la diligencia es madre de la buena ventura" (C-044c) are given from general knowledge and are not in our corpus notes. Resolved (repair 5): the Gellius reference is kept as a primary reference; the truchas proverb is deleted; the diligence saying is now taken only from Barros's own "como algunos dizen".
11. **Printer.** C-069b identifies "Iosep Cacchij" as Giuseppe Cacchi, also from general knowledge; confirm against EDIT16 or similar before publishing. Resolved (repair 5): the note now gives the imprint's form and Lucero's Italian form, cited (Dedicatoria, p. 47); EDIT16 not consulted.
12. **Consistency of "diligencia(s)".** Rendered as "diligent steps", "steps", "efforts" or "pains" depending on context, and as "Diligence" for the square (20); the Diligence pun is noted where the text plays on it (C-044c, C-049b). The other translator may wish to align.
