ES: POR quanto por parte de vos Alonso de Barros, nuestro criado, nos ha sido hecha relacion, que vos aueys compuesto vna pintura intitulada Filosofia Cortesana, con ciertas differencias de figuras y letras que se contiene en un pliego grande, y la aueys moralizado en vna relacion a parte[.?] Y nos suplicastes, que teniendo consideracion a lo que en ello aueys trabajado, os diessemos licencia, y ma[n]dassemos que vos, o la persona, ò personas que vuestro poder huuieren, lo pedays[?] imprimir, y ve[n]der enestos nuestros reynos, y no otras algunas, o como la nuestra merced fuesse: Y porque auiendose visto por nuestro mandado, parecio ser obra de mucho ingenio, y que serà vtil à la Republica, por ser de honesto y gustoso entretenimiento. Por la presente os damos licencia y facultad, para que por tiempo de diez años primeros siguientes, contados desde el dia de la fecha desta nuestra cedula en adelante, vos a la persona, o personas, que para ello vuestro poder huuieren, podays imprimir y ve[n]der enestos nuestros Reynos y señorios la dicha pintura, y moralidad della. Y ma[n]damos, que otras personas algunas no lo puedan imprimir, ni vender, ni imprima[·] , ni vendan enestos dichos nuestros reynos y señorios, so pena que qualesquier personas que sin tener el dicho vuestro poder lo imprimieren, o hizieren imprimir, o lo vendieren, o hizieren vender, pierdan toda la impression que hizieren i vendieren, i los moldes y aparejos con que lo hizieren, i mas incurran por cada vez en pena de cinquenta mil marauedis. La tercia parte para nuestra camara. Y la tercia parte para el Iuez que lo sentenciare. Y la otra tercia parte para el denunciador. Y mandamos, que cada pliego de la dicha pintura con la dicha moralidad, se venda al precio que por los del nuestro Consejo fuere tassado. A los quales, y a los Presidentes, y Oydores de las nuestras audiencias, y a otros qualesquier juezes y justicias destos dichos nuestros Reynos y señorios, mandamos assimismo, que guarden y cumplan, y hagan guardar y cumplir esta nuestra cedula, y lo en ella contenido. Fecha en Madrid a nueue de Febrero, de mil y quinientos y ochenta y siete años.
EN: Whereas, on behalf of you, Alonso de Barros, our servant, report has been made to us that you have composed a picture entitled *Filosofía cortesana*, with a number of different figures and verses, which is contained on one large sheet, and that you have written a moral explanation of it in a separate account; and you begged us that, taking into consideration the labor you have put into it, we should give you license, and order that you, or the person or persons holding your power of attorney, and no other persons whatever, might print and sell it in these our kingdoms, or as our pleasure might be. And because, having been examined by our order, it appeared to be a work of great ingenuity, and one that will be useful to the commonwealth, being honest and pleasant entertainment: by these presents we give you license and authority so that, for the term of the ten years next following, counted from the day of the date of this our warrant onward, you or the person or persons holding your power for it may print and sell in these our kingdoms and lordships the said picture, and its moral explanation. And we order that no other persons whatever may print it or sell it, nor print nor sell it in these our said kingdoms and lordships, on pain that any persons who, without holding your said power, should print it or cause it to be printed, or sell it or cause it to be sold, shall lose the whole impression that they make and sell, and the formes and equipment with which they make it, and shall further incur, for each offense, a penalty of fifty thousand maravedís. One third for our Chamber. And one third for the judge who passes sentence. And the other third for the informer. And we order that each sheet of the said picture, with the said moral explanation, be sold at the price that shall be set by those of our Council. To whom, and to the Presidents and Judges (*oidores*) of our Audiencias, and to any other judges and officers of justice whatever of these our said kingdoms and lordships, we likewise order that they keep and fulfill, and cause to be kept and fulfilled, this our warrant and what it contains. Given in Madrid on the ninth of February, in the year one thousand five hundred and eighty-seven.
NOTE: (1) *Pintura* is the printed board, "one large sheet" (*pliego grande*); the *relacion a parte* / *moralidad* is this book. The privilege licenses both, and prices them together "each sheet of the said picture with the said moral explanation". *Letras* here are the inscriptions/verses on the board. (2) *lo pedays[?] imprimir* is rendered as *podays* ("might print"); the transcription notes the e may be an ink-filled o. (3) *vos a la persona* (pdf 10) is rendered "you or the person": the printed a is probably an ink-filled o, and the parallel formula on pdf 9 has *o*. (4) *ni imprima[·] , ni vendan*: the n (or tilde) of *impriman* failed to print; the doubled "print … sell … nor print nor sell" is the legal formula, kept. (5) *moldes y aparejos*: the type set up in formes, and the press equipment. (6) *nuestra camara*: the royal treasury (the Cámara). *Consejo*: the royal Council, which set the official price (*tasa*) per sheet. *Oydores*: the judges of the Audiencias, the high courts. (7) *Cedula*: a royal warrant (*cédula*). The maravedí was the Castilian money of account. (8) *y no otras algunas*: feminine, so "no other persons", not "no other kingdoms"; the privilege repeats the restriction below ("otras personas algunas no lo puedan imprimir").

::: seg id=C-014c pdf=14 page= sig=A4v kind=verse squares=
ES: REduzir a plazer la pesadumbre / De pretensiones; que consumen vidas; / Aprender a ganar: y a ver perdidas / Las esperancas con incierta lumbre: / Mirar como arrojadas de la cumbre, / Quanto mas leuantadas: mas caydas / Estan nuestras venturas reduzidas / Al fallo de ambiciosa seruidumbre; / Esta filosofia no hallada / En el discurso de la edad primera / Que tuuo sus desseos limitados. / Estaua a vuestra pluma reseruada, / Come[?] si de Platon regida fuera; / Para norte de gusto: y de cuydados[.?]
EN: To turn to pleasure all the heaviness / of petitions for office, which consume lives; / to learn to win, and to behold as lost / the hopes, in an uncertain light; / to see how, flung down from the very summit, / the higher they were raised, the further fallen, / our fortunes stand reduced / to the sentence of ambitious servitude: / this philosophy, which was never found / in the course of that first age of the world / which held its own desires within their bounds, / was kept in reserve for your pen, / as if it had been governed by Plato, / to be a pole-star both for pleasure and for cares.
NOTE: Line-by-line version, unrhymed.<!-- The sonnets are not among the guide's fixed verses, so no LIT/VID. --> The whole sonnet is one period: the infinitives (*REduzir … Aprender a ganar: y a ver … Mirar*) stand in apposition to "this philosophy", which is the subject of *Estaua … reseruada*. (1) *pesadumbre*: both "weight" and "grief"; "heaviness" keeps both. *REduzir a plazer* is echoed by *reduzidas* (l. 7), and Barros's "Al lector" says his aim was to "reduzir a orden" the business of petitioning. (2) *pretensiones*: petitions for office or favor (guide: *pretensión* = petition). (3) *ganar … perdidas*: gaming words, win and lose, for the book is a game. (4) *con incierta lumbre* may go with *ver* (to see by an uncertain light) or with *esperancas* (hopes that shine uncertainly); the English "in an uncertain light" keeps both. *esperancas* is so printed, without cedilla. (5) *de la cumbre*: from the summit of favor; the rise and fall is the image of Fortune's wheel. (8) *fallo*: a judgment or verdict (legal sense, rendered "sentence"). In card play *fallo* is also being void in a suit, and *fallar* is "to fail"; a gaming pun may be intended, but the evidence here does not settle it. (9–11) *la edad primera*: the first age of classical myth (the Golden Age), when people kept their desires within bounds and there were no petitions and no court; so the "philosophy" of petitioning had no occasion to be found. *discurso*: the course (of time), also "discourse", the word Barros uses for his own "discurso de pretensores". (12) *vuestra pluma*: Barros's pen (the sonnet addresses the author). (13) *Come[?]* is blotted in this copy; read *Como*, as in Wilson's transcription of the Madrid 1587 (Madrigal) printing and in the Lemir text of this Naples copy. Plato stands as the philosopher par excellence: Barros's "philosophy" is as well ordered as if Plato had governed it. No particular dialogue need be meant. The modernized transcription of the Madrid 1587 text by Luigi Ciompi and Adrian Seville (giochidelloca.it) reads "como de Platón regida fuera", dropping *si*; that line is a syllable short, and the page image has *si*. (14) *norte*: the pole-star, the navigator's guide, the image that Cervantes's sonnet on the facing page develops into a whole sea. The final stop after *cuydados* is blotted.

::: seg id=C-015c pdf=15 page= sig=A5r kind=verse squares=
ES: QVal vemos del rozado y rico Oriente; / La blanca y dura piedra señalarse[?] / Y en todo (aunque pequenna[?]) auentajarse / A la mayor del Caucaso eminente. / Tal este humilde al parecer presente / Puede: y deue mirarse: y admirarse: / No por la cantidad; mas por mostrarse / Ser en su calidad tan excelente. / El que nauega por el golfo insano / Del mar de pretensiones; vera al punto / Del cortesano laberintio el hilo. / Felice ingenio: y venturosa mano / Quel deleyte: y prouecho puso iunto / En juego alegre, en dulce y claro estilo;
EN: As we see, from the rosy and rich East, / the white and hard stone distinguish itself, / and in every way (though it is small) surpass / the greatest of the towering Caucasus, / so this present, humble as it seems, / can, and should, be looked upon and wondered at, / not for its quantity, but for showing / itself to be so excellent in quality. / Whoever sails across the maddened gulf / of the sea of petitions will see, upon the instant, / the thread of the labyrinth of the court. / Happy the wit, and fortunate the hand, / that put delight and profit together / in a merry game, in sweet and lucid style.
NOTE: Line-by-line version, unrhymed.<!-- No LIT/VID (not a fixed verse). --> Structure: *Qual … Tal* ("As … so"): an octave of comparison (the small gem, the small book), then a tercet on what the book shows the petitioner, then a tercet of praise. (1) *rozado* is the Naples spelling; the Madrid 1587 (Madrigal) printing, in Wilson's facsimile, reads *rosado*, "rosy", and that is the sense: the East rosy with dawn, the East of rich gems. (2) *La blanca y dura piedra*: a white, hard stone from the East, which, though small, outdoes the greatest of the Caucasus. The hardness points to the diamond (*diamante*, the "adamant" of the ancients), prized for its hardness and water-clear whiteness; the finest came from the East (India). A pearl is possible: pearls were counted among the precious stones, and *oriente* is also the jeweler's word for a pearl's luster. But a pearl is not "hard". The notes consulted do not discuss the point; the translation keeps "stone" and leaves the gem unnamed, as Cervantes does. *señalarse[?]*: the letters after *se-* are blotted; read *señalarse* (to stand out, distinguish itself), as in the Madrid 1587 facsimile, and Schevill and Bonilla printed *sennalarse* from this Naples copy (as Wilson reports). (3) *pequenna[?]*: two n-forms or an ñ run into a blot; the word is *pequeña*, "small" (Madrid 1587 has *pequeña*; Schevill and Bonilla print *pequenna* from this copy: the Neapolitan printer used nn for ñ). (4) *A la mayor del Caucaso eminente*: "the greatest [stone] of the lofty Caucasus". The Caucasus is the classical byword for towering mountains (Prometheus was chained to one of its crags), so the comparison is between the gem and a whole mountain of rock: value against bulk. *eminente* is both "towering" and a word of rank. (5) *presente*: "gift" and "here present"; the English "present" keeps both. The gift is the book offered to Mateo Vázquez, a small duodecimo; the octave turns on quantity against quality (*cantidad … calidad*), the book's smallness against its worth, a point the dedication itself makes ("tan pequeña obra en las palabras, y tan gra[n]de en la substancia"). (6) *mirarse: y admirarse*: a jingle, "looked at and wondered at", kept as closely as English allows. *Ser* (l. 8) has a blotted initial. (9–10) *el golfo insano / Del mar de pretensiones*: *golfo* is the open sea far from shore (compare Barros's opening, "no se engolfando en alta mar"); *insano* is "mad", "raging", and also "unhealthy". The "sea of petitions" is the sea at the center of the board (the Sea of Suffering), which Bidwell-Steiner (2024) sees illustrated there. The modernized transcription of the Madrid 1587 text by Ciompi and Seville reads "por golfo insano", dropping *el*; that line is a syllable short, and both the page image and the other two witnesses (the Madrid 1587 facsimile and the Lemir text of this copy) have *el*. *al punto*: "at once". A glance at the dice's *puntos* (points) is possible in a poem about a game, but the notes consulted do not settle it. (11) *Del cortesano laberintio el hilo*: Ariadne's thread, which led Theseus out of the Cretan labyrinth; here the book is the thread through the labyrinth of the court. *laberintio* is so printed here and in Madrid 1587; it is not a misreading. Collar de Cáceres (2009) notes that "labyrinth" was also a name for such race games, so the court-labyrinth is also the board. (12) *Felice*: an Italianate or poetic form of *feliz*. *venturosa mano*: the "lucky hand" of the author, with a possible glance at the game, where a *mano* is a round of play. (13–14) *Quel* = *que el*. Joining "delight and profit" in a "sweet" style is the Horatian precept (*Ars poetica* 343) that the poet who mixes the useful with the sweet wins every vote; the sonnet ends by praising the book in those terms.

ES: MAteria es esta del pretender, donde qua[n]do todos los escritores de buen entendimiento, que ha[n] auido, y ay oy en el mu[n]do, quisieran mostrarse, por mucho que huuieran continuado la pluma quedaran cortos, segun los altos y baxos de prospera, y aduersa fortuna, co[n]tentos, y quexas de agrauios que en ella ay. Y quando yo lo quede, quedare disculpado, pues[?] mi intento no ha sido sino[?] mostrar entre burla[?] y juego, las veras[?] y desuenturas que siguen a vna larga pretension, y reduzir a orden lo que tan sin ella se trata. El que con mas curiosidad[?] lo quisiere mirar, muestre su habilidad en hazer otra cosa mejor, y no ponga mucho estudio en notar sus faltas, que en ninguna de las de letras humanas dexa[n] de hallarlas los ojos cabilosos de los que las miran. Y es de creer, que todos como yo, han desseado acertar, y no por esso se han librado deste peligro. Esta consideracion disculpa mi atreuimiento, con que si la obra no fuere tal, como yo quisiera, su breuedad la assegura de ser fastidiosa, que de lo malo, no es lo peor.
EN: This matter of petitioning for advancement is one in which, even if all the writers of good understanding who have ever been, and who are in the world today, wished to show their powers, however long they had kept their pens going they would fall short, given the highs and lows of prosperous and adverse fortune, the satisfactions, and the complaints of injuries that there are in it. And if I fall short of it, I shall be excused, since my intention has been nothing but to show, in jest and in play, the earnest truths and misfortunes that follow long petitioning, and to reduce to order what is handled so entirely without it. Whoever should wish to look at it more curiously, let him show his skill by making something better, and not put much study into noting its faults; for in no work of humane letters do the captious eyes of those who look at them fail to find them. And it is to be believed that all, like me, have wished to hit the mark, and not for that have they been spared this danger. This consideration excuses my boldness; so that, if the work should not be such as I would wish, its brevity keeps it from being tiresome, which, of bad things, is not the worst.
NOTE: (1) *pretender*: to seek office or favor at court; first occurrence of the verb in the book, rendered "petitioning for advancement" (guide). (2) *entre burla y juego, las veras*: the Spanish pair *burlas / veras*, "jest" and "earnest": the game is play, what it shows is in earnest. The upper lines of pdf 17 are slurred and blotted, and *pues[?]*, *sino[?]*, *burla[?]*, *veras[?]* and *curiosidad[?]* are read from letter outlines; the sense of the passage is secure. (3) *reduzir a orden*: compare Liñán's sonnet, "REduzir a plazer". (4) *curiosidad*: care, minute scrutiny. (5) *de las de letras humanas* is so printed, with an extra *de* (Ciompi and Seville's modernized transcription of the Madrid 1587 text has "de las letras humanas"); rendered as the evident sense, "in no work of humane letters". *cabilosos*: "cavilling", captious. (6) The last clause is proverbial in shape: brevity is a merit even in a bad work, since a bad thing that is short is not the worst kind of bad thing. *disculpa-|do* is divided across the page and joined in ES.

ES: *A MATEO VAZQVEZ de Leca, del conseio de su Magestad, y su secretario, y de la santa general Inquisicion, arcediano de Carmona, y canonigo en la santa yglesia de Seuilla.*

ES: *CAminase por el Trabajo adelante, hasta que a fuerça de mohinas, y desengaños, da el pretensor en el miedo (del que diran) que es nacido, de yr cayendo en la cue[n]ta de su perdicion, quando halla trocadas sus esperanças de la figura en q[ue] al principio las tuuo, y que le na[sic] faltando el caudal para assistir en lo començado: y para boluer a su tierra, esta con vn temor grandissimo (del que diran) si buelue maniuazio, segnn[sic] las muchas prendas que para ello ha puesto: lo qual le haze estar suspenso en su determinacion hasta que la toma de dar de mano a este miedo y hazer lo que mejor pudiere: y para que assi lo haga, dize la letra.*
EN: One travels on through Labor until, by dint of vexations and disillusions, the petitioner lands in the fear (of What Will They Say?), which is born of his coming, little by little, to realize his ruin, when he finds his hopes changed from the shape in which he held them at the beginning, and that his means are running short for staying on in what he began. And as for going back to his own country, he is in the greatest dread (of what they will say) if he returns empty-handed, after the many pledges he has put up for it. This keeps him in suspense in his resolve, until he takes the resolution to wave this fear aside and do the best he can; and so that he may do so, the verse says:
NOTE: (1) *CAminase por el Trabajo adelante*: impersonal, "one travels on through Labor", along the track with its Labor squares. (2) *mohinas*: vexations, sulks. *desengaños*: disillusions (guide). (3) *el miedo (del que diran)*: the square of What Will They Say? (36); in the second occurrence the parenthesis is the ordinary phrase "of what they will say", and the English marks the difference by capitals. Barros gives no number. (4) *yr cayendo en la cue[n]ta de su perdicion*: *caer en la cuenta*, "to realize", literally "to fall into the reckoning" (a *cuenta* is also an account): the petitioner's ruin is being totted up. (5) *le na faltando* is so printed, for *le va faltando*, "is running short". *el caudal*: one's capital, one's funds. *assistir*: to stay in attendance at court. (6) *maniuazio*: *manivacío*, "empty-handed", printed as one word; *segnn* is so printed (turned u, for *segun*). (7) *las muchas prendas que para ello ha puesto*: *prendas* are pledges, the things one puts up as security or as stakes; the petitioner has pledged much (goods, credit, reputation) on returning with something. (8) *dar de mano a este miedo*: *dar de mano*, "to push away with the hand", to dismiss; a play on *maniuazio* is possible. *di-|ran* is divided across the page, joined in ES.

ES: BVelue a los dados veyntiocho, a buscar otra suerte de negociar, mas como para el desdichado, que en todo quanto mano pone halla azar, le seria la mejor suerte vn breue desengaño. Esta[n] pintados con vn azar de menor, y vna letra que dize.
EN: He goes back to the Dice, at twenty-eight, to try his luck at another way of pressing his case; but, as for the unlucky man who finds a losing throw in everything he turns his hand to, the best luck for him would be a quick disillusion. They are painted with a losing throw of the lowest kind, and a verse that says:
NOTE: (1) ES joins the end of pdf 37 (part 1 of the transcription, which ends at *halla*) with the first paragraph of pdf 38 (part 2, which begins *azar,*). The italic markup is omitted in this block because part 2 does not mark the typeface. (2) *BVelue a los dados veyntiocho*: *volver a* is "to go back to" and "to turn again to": the narrative goes back from 36 to the Dice at 28 (Barros does not take the squares in order), and the petitioner turns again to the dice, to gamble. The English "goes back to" is meant to carry both. (3) *otra suerte de negociar*: *suerte* is "sort, kind" and "luck" and "a throw of the dice" (guide: pun); the English "try his luck at another way" keeps luck and kind; the throw is in "the Dice". (4) *halla azar*: finds a losing throw, finds bad luck (guide: *azar*). *mano* ("hand") is also a round of play (guide: *mano*), set beside *azar*. (5) *le seria la mejor suerte vn breue desengaño*: the best "throw" would be a swift disillusionment (*desengaño*), i.e. to give up. (6) *Esta[n] pintados*: "they [the dice] are painted". *vn azar de menor*: a losing throw "of the lower [number]"; the board shows three dice showing one, two and three pips, which Zollinger reads as a minor throw: {q|GameGooseLargest|pdf0|web|une chance mineure, les trois dés offrant 1, 2 et 3 points} (a minor chance, the three dice showing 1, 2 and 3 points) [@GameGooseLargest]. The exact technical sense of *azar de menor* in the dice games of the period is not established in the notes consulted; "a losing throw of the lowest kind" is the likely sense.
