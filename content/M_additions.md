# Madrigal (M) passages absent from or different in the Naples text (C): English translation

**Witness.** M = *Filosofía cortesana moralizada*, Madrid: Pedro Madrigal, 1587 (Cambridge, Emmanuel College, Adams B. 253). We have no image of M.

**Whose Spanish this is.** Except for the title page (M-01r-a, whose source is given there), every Spanish passage of M on this page is copied from the modernized transcription of the Madrid 1587 text made by Luigi Ciompi and Adrian Seville for their catalog *Il gioco dell'Oca* on giochidelloca.it (http://www.giochidelloca.it/storia/filosofia.pdf), which they made from Trevor J. Dadson's 1987 edition [@sanchezEdicionesAntiguasFilosofia2016oct16, 173 n. 14; @debarrosFilosofiaCortesana1587]. That Spanish text is their work, not this edition's, and it is not covered by this edition's public-domain dedication. The English translation of M given here was made by this edition from their transcription. Its spelling, accents and punctuation are modern, not Madrigal's, and it contains the transcribers' slips as well as M's readings; Lucero Sánchez, who describes it, warns that it has many errors [@sanchezEdicionesAntiguasFilosofia2016oct16, 173 n. 14]. The bracketed folio markers ("[42r]") are the transcription's own. They mark Madrigal folios but are irregular (repeated and out of order in places).<!-- Maintainers: the transcription is held in the research corpus as `corpus/debarrosFilosofiaCortesana1587.md`, every ES line below is copied mechanically from it, conversion artifacts (page anchors, stray "Notes" headings) have been removed and line breaks joined, and nothing else is changed. Because the folio markers are irregular, the `fol=` values are approximate. -->

**A hybrid text.** The transcription opens with the *Naples* title page ("En Nápoles por Iosep Cacchÿ. 1588") and ends with the Madrigal colophon. Its text is Madrigal's: it has the Fortune additions and the Declaración, which C lacks [@sanchezEdicionesAntiguasFilosofia2016oct16, 173 n. 14, 179–80, 189–90; @sanchezFILOSOFIACORTESANAALONSO, 17].

**Scope.** Every passage where the transcription of M is absent from the Naples text (C), or differs from it in sense, checked against Lucero's M/G collation tables. Differences in spelling and word order, and places where the transcription is demonstrably in error, are not given here, so that the site does not present them as Madrigal readings.<!-- Maintainers: the passages were found from the variant lists at the end of `notes/barros1588_transcription_part1.md` and `_part2.md`; the excluded differences are listed in the table at the end of this file. -->

::: seg id=M-01r-a fol=1r kind=prose replaces=C-007c squares=none
ES: FILOSOFIA | Cortesana, moralizada por | Alonso de Barros, criado del | Rey nuestro señor. | Dirigida a Mateo Vazquez de | Leca, del consejo de su Magestad | y su secretario, y de la santa gene- | ral Inquisicion, arcediano de Car | mona, y canonigo en la santa | yglesia de Sevilla. | Tassado en medio Real.
EN: THE COURTLY PHILOSOPHY, moralized by Alonso de Barros, servant of the King our lord. Dedicated to Mateo Vázquez de Leca, of His Majesty's Council and his secretary, and of the Holy General Inquisition, archdeacon of Carmona and canon in the holy church of Seville. Priced at half a real.
NOTE: ES here is not from Ciompi and Seville's transcription, which opens with the Naples title page ("En Nápoles por Iosep Cacchÿ. 1588") and so has no text of the Madrigal title. ES is copied from Lucero's line-for-line transcription of the Madrigal title page [@sanchezEdicionesAntiguasFilosofia2016oct16, 179] (Wilson's facsimile of the Emmanuel copy's title page, Fig. 1, reads "Seuilla" [@wilsonCervantesItemEmmanuel1968, 366]). How M differs from C: C's title (C-007c–C-007h, pdf 7) sets FILOSOFIA / CORTESANA / MORALIZADA in capitals, has "sancta general Inquisicion", and ends with the imprint "En Napoles por Iosep Cacchij" (no date). M prints no imprint on the title page. Instead it has Mateo Vázquez's arms in an oval woodcut with the motto IN HOC SIGNO VINCES, and the official price ("Tassado en medio Real"); both are absent from C [@sanchezEdicionesAntiguasFilosofia2016oct16, 179–80; @wilsonCervantesItemEmmanuel1968, 366]. "criado del Rey" = a member of the royal household (Barros was a royal aposentador). "moralizada" is kept as "moralized" because the book presents itself as the moral reading of the picture.
:::

::: seg id=M-12v-a fol=12v kind=prose replaces=C-026a squares=1
ES: El que comienza a pretender entra por la puerta de la opinión, engañado de su pensamiento con la estimación propia y satisfacción que de sus valedores tiene;
EN: Whoever begins to petition for advancement enters by the Gate of Opinion, deceived by his own thinking, with the self-esteem and the confidence he has in his patrons;
NOTE: C (pdf 26) reads "engañado de pensamiento" ("deceived by fancy", with no possessive); the transcription adds "su" ("his own"). In English the change is slight: C's bare "pensamiento" can mean imagination in general; "su pensamiento" makes the delusion the petitioner's own. Lucero's G/M collation tables do not cite this phrase, but Martínez Millán, quoting M (fols. 12v–13v), reads "engañado de su pensamiento con la estimación propia" [@millanFilosofiaCortesanaAlonso1996, 468], so "su" is Madrigal's reading, not only the modern transcriber's. The rest of the paragraph (peacock, swan) agrees with C in sense and is left to the C translators.
:::

::: seg id=M-17r-a fol=17r kind=verse replaces=C-032f squares=41
ES: Trabajo es no le tener el que de él ha de comer.
EN: To have no work is work indeed / for one who works to meet his need.
LITERAL: It is hardship not to have it [work], for one who must eat by it.
NOTE: Fixed translation t6. C (pdf 32) prints "Trabajo no le tener; / El que del ha de comer." with no "es"; the verb is supplied in the fixed literal, so one English text serves both editions. Lucero's M column has the "es" ("Trabajo es no le tener / el que dél ha de comer"), so it is a genuine Madrigal reading, not only the modern transcriber's [@sanchezEdicionesAntiguasFilosofia2016oct16, 188]; the Spanish on the Naples board (square 41) also has "es". The transcription prints this couplet twice in a row. That is a transcription slip: nine couplets here would contradict "sin la primera otras ocho casas", and Lucero's M list has it once. Pun: *trabajo* = both "work" and "hardship".
:::

::: seg id=M-17r-b fol=17r–17v kind=prose replaces=C-032i squares=15
ES: Luego, a quince casas, se pone el paso de la esperanza, que es con la que se anima el nuevo pretendiente [17v] cuando, visitando a sus amigos y valedores y habiéndoles dado cuenta de su negocio, ellos le responden con tan gran encarecimiento de palabras y exageración de sus méritos
EN: Then, at fifteen houses, is placed the Pass of Hope. This is the hope that cheers the new petitioner when, visiting his friends and patrons and having told them about his case, they answer him with such lavish words and such exaggeration of his merits
NOTE: C (pdf 32–33) reads "visitando a sus amigos y auiendoles dado cuenta": friends only, no "y valedores" ("and patrons"). The transcription adds the patrons, so the new petitioner's round of visits takes in not only friends but the people who might speak for the petitioner. Lucero's collation cites the M wording of the next clause ("ellos le responden con tan gran encarecimiento"; G "le responden con gran encarescimiento") but not this phrase [@sanchezEdicionesAntiguasFilosofia2016oct16, 184]. So the sources we cite do not settle whether "y valedores" is Madrigal's or the transcriber's (it may also come from Dadson's text, on which the transcription depends). *pretendiente* = petitioner (office-seeker).
:::

::: seg id=M-19r-a fol=19r–19v kind=prose replaces=C-035b squares=32
ES: El que cayere en el pozo ha menester sogas de liberalidad para salir, ganando la voluntad de los que algo pueden con el valedor, para que le acuerden su negocio. Y así se le manda que, habiendo estado olvidado [19v] dando una mano, dé para ellas un tanto a cada unos de los jugadores y dos en la polla.
EN: Whoever falls into the Well needs ropes of liberality to get out, winning the goodwill of those who have some sway with the patron, so that they will remind him of his case. And so he is ordered, having stayed forgotten while a round is played, to give for them [the ropes] one stake to each of the players and two into the pot.
NOTE: C (pdf 35–36) reads "auiendo estado oluidado vna mano" ("having stayed forgotten one round"). The transcription adds "dando" ("dando una mano", literally "giving a round"; in card play *dar una mano* is to play or deal a round). Either way the sense is to miss one round, as the rules state ("habiendo estado sin jugar una mano", M-47r-a). Martínez Millán, quoting M itself (fols. 18v–19r), has no "dando": {q|millanFilosofiaCortesanaAlonso1996|pdf18|478|auiendo estado oluidado una mano, dé para ellas}; the word is therefore the modern transcription's, not Madrigal's. "a cada unos" (sic) in the transcription; C "a cada vno". Lucero records G "Y ansí" against M "Y así se le manda que" [@sanchezEdicionesAntiguasFilosofia2016oct16, 186] without saying which passage it belongs to; if it is this one, G lacked "se le manda que" ("he is ordered to").
:::

::: seg id=M-37r-a fol=37r kind=prose replaces=C-061a squares=51
ES: Pónese sobre un globo que es el universo, por el dominio y superioridad que muestra tener sobre todo lo criado que es corruptible. Y de pies sobre una bola, que con facilidad se vuelve lo de arriba abajo, significando la poca firmeza que promete en lo que da.
EN: She is set upon a globe, which is the universe, for the dominion and superiority she shows over all created things that are corruptible. And she stands upon a ball, on which what is on top is easily turned to the bottom, signifying how little constancy she promises in what she gives.
NOTE: The first sentence is shared with C (C-061a, pdf 61) and is given only for context. The second sentence ("Y de pies sobre una bola…") is absent from C, which goes straight on to "Pintase desnuda" (C-061b). This passage describes the Fortune woodcut that M alone prints (fol. 36r). Wilson reproduces the block (his Fig. 3): a winged Fortune standing on a single sphere, within the inscription NO SERIA FORTVNA SI FVESE SIEMPRE VNA [@wilsonCervantesItemEmmanuel1968, 366, 368]; Collar de Cáceres describes her "de pie sobre una esfera" [@decaceresTableroItalianoFilosofia2009, 90]. The woodcut shows one sphere, not a globe and a separate ball. C keeps the whole description of Fortune but not the woodcut, so in C the passage describes an image that the book does not contain. G has neither the woodcut nor the description: it goes from the couplet "No sería Fortuna" directly to the rules [@sanchezEdicionesAntiguasFilosofia2016oct16, 188 and n. 57]. "de pies" = standing (on her feet). Fortune on the board (square 51) stands on a sphere.
:::

::: seg id=M-38v-a fol=38v–39r kind=prose replaces=C-063b squares=51
ES: Tiene dos caras, de que usa con los unos mansa, benigna y favorable, cortada a medida de su deseo, de manera que, tan soberbios como ingratos, con la mucha prosperidad, olvidados totalmente de su condición, la niegan el poder, atribuyendo a su propio [39v] valor la gloria que de ella reciben. Y con los otros se muestra tan contraria, áspera y terrible que, aunque con humildad la reconocen en todo por señora, no la han visto alegre en el discurso de la vida.
EN: She has two faces, which she uses: with some she is gentle, kind and favorable, cut to the measure of their desire, so that, as proud as they are ungrateful in their great prosperity, having wholly forgotten their condition, they deny her power and ascribe to their own worth the glory they receive from her. And with the others she shows herself so contrary, harsh and terrible that, although they humbly acknowledge her as mistress in everything, they have never seen her cheerful in the whole course of their life.
NOTE: C (pdf 63) has only: "Tiene dos caras de que usa, con los vnos mansa, benigna, y fauorable, cortada a medida de su desseo: y con los otros tan contraria, aspera y terrible, que no la han visto alegre en el discurso de su vida." M adds two clauses: the favored, proud and ungrateful, credit their own worth; the unfavored acknowledge her humbly and still never see her smile. C also reads "de su vida" where the transcription has "de la vida"; Martínez Millán and Collar de Cáceres, both quoting M, read "de su vida" [@millanFilosofiaCortesanaAlonso1996, 474; @decaceresTableroItalianoFilosofia2009, 90], so "de la vida" is the transcription's slip (no difference in English). "la niegan el poder": *laísmo* in the transcription, "they deny her [her] power". "cortada a medida de su deseo" = "cut to measure", a tailoring image, kept.
:::

::: seg id=M-39r-a fol=39r–39v kind=prose replaces=C-063b squares=51
ES: Pusieron la copete y caballos sólo en la frente, avisando de la diligencia y cuidado con que debemos estar [39v], para no perder la ocasión que se ofrece, antes que con arrebatada vuelta muestre su calva cabeza y deje burlados a los que pudiendo no la asieron y se aprovecharon de ella.
EN: They put the forelock and hair on her forehead only, warning of the diligence and care with which we must keep watch so as not to lose the opportunity that offers itself, before she turns suddenly and shows her bald head, leaving mocked those who could have seized her and did not, and did not make use of her.
NOTE: The whole paragraph is absent from C; it would fall between "Tiene dos caras" (C-063b) and "Tiene en la mano derecha" (C-063c), pdf 63. It gives Fortune the attributes of Occasion, the bald woman with a forelock whom the corner of the board shows reclining with "No me pierdas" ("Don't lose me") and whom Barros's epilogue explains ("una mujer con un manojo de cabellos en la frente y la cabeza calva, que significa la ocasión"). "caballos" ("horses") in the transcription is evidently a slip for "cabellos" ("hair"), and is translated so; "Pusieron la" = *Pusiéronla*, "they put on her" (Martínez Millán, quoting M, reads "Pusieronla copete y cabellos solo en la frente" [@millanFilosofiaCortesanaAlonso1996, 474]; Collar de Cáceres, "Pusieronla copete cabellos" [@decaceresTableroItalianoFilosofia2009, 90]); *copete* is the object. "Pusieron" has no expressed subject: "they", i.e. whoever devised the image (the paragraph that opens the description of Fortune speaks of "los antiguos": "a quien los antiguos llamaron Fortuna"). "a los que pudiendo no la asieron y se aprovecharon de ella": the negative governs both verbs ("did not seize her and [did not] make use of her"). Wilson's summary of the M description also notes that Fortune, like Occasion, has hair only in a forelock [@wilsonCervantesItemEmmanuel1968, 368–69].
:::

::: seg id=M-41v-a fol=41v kind=rules replaces=C-066a squares=none
ES: Declaración del juego y orden de jugarle
EN: Explanation of the Game, and the Order of Playing It
NOTE: Heading of M's final section (Wilson: D5v, "Declaraciõ del juego, y orden de jugarle" [@wilsonCervantesItemEmmanuel1968, 365]). C (C-066a, pdf 66) heads its much shorter rules "EL YVEGO SE juega en esta forma." ("The game is played in this manner"), close to G's "Esto se juega en esta forma" ("This is played in this manner") [@sanchezEdicionesAntiguasFilosofia2016oct16, 189]. Everything from here to M-46r-a is absent from C and from G. Lucero says that M's opening "hardly coincides at all" with G's, and that M then digresses on the board, some of its emblems and a worked example before the two editions converge again on the principal squares [@sanchezEdicionesAntiguasFilosofia2016oct16, 189 n. 58].
:::

::: seg id=M-41v-b fol=41v–42r kind=rules replaces=C-066b squares=none
ES: Las pesadumbres del pretender son muchas, y aunque para su reparo fuera necesario mayor remedio, el que se ofrece ha sido hacerle del juego, del cual se ha hecho relación hasta aquí. Y para mejor entenderse hubiera de ir junto con el libro. Pero porque no estorbe al [42r] uso de jugarse, anda aparte en un papel grande pintado con 63 casas o divisiones, que son los años de la vida que se gastan en una pretensión y los que también la gastan a ella.
EN: The griefs of petitioning are many, and although a greater remedy would be needed to relieve them, the one offered has been to make a game of it, the game of which an account has been given up to here. To be better understood it ought to go together with the book; but so that it does not get in the way of the game being played, it comes separately, on a large sheet painted with 63 houses or divisions. These are the years of life that are spent in a petition, and also the years that wear the petition itself out.
NOTE: Not in C or G. Lucero quotes this sentence as "el que se ofrece ha sido hacerlo juego" ("the one on offer has been to turn it into a game") [@sanchezEdicionesAntiguasFilosofia2016oct16, 189], where the transcription reads "hacerle del juego"; the translation follows the transcription, and the sense is the same either way: the remedy is the game. "el pretender": Barros's nominalized infinitive ("petitioning", "seeking advancement"), which Lucero takes as a mark of the same hand as the prologue ("Materia es ésta del pretender") [@sanchezEdicionesAntiguasFilosofia2016oct16, 183]. Pun on *gastar*: the years "que se gastan en una pretensión" (spent on a petition) and "los que también la gastan a ella" (that wear it out, i.e. outlast and consume it); English "spend / wear out" keeps both halves. "papel grande pintado" = a large printed sheet (Barros's "pintura"); the license calls it "un pliego grande". "casas" = houses (this edition's squares).
:::

::: seg id=M-42r-a fol=42r–42v kind=rules replaces=C-066b squares=1
ES: Tiene en la puerta de la primera casa un pavo real, que cuando se considera llevar el carro de la diosa Juno, significa grandeza, y cuando con gallardía hace la rueda, significa opinión. Y para desengaño del que, en hacerla, algunos reciben, está sobre [42v] ésta un cisne denunciador de su propio fin, y aquí lo es del ajeno, con que el tan celebrado dicho que los antiguos tuvieron por palabras caídas del cielo: “Conócete a ti mismo”, avisando de lo que nos conviene en todo.
EN: At the door of the first house there is a peacock, which, when it is thought of as drawing the chariot of the goddess Juno, signifies greatness, and, when it spreads its tail into a wheel with a flourish, signifies Opinion. And to undo the self-deception that some fall into in spreading it, above this one there is a swan, herald of its own end, and here the herald of another's; with which [comes] the much-celebrated saying that the ancients held to be words fallen from heaven, "Know thyself", warning of what is fitting for us in all things.
NOTE: Not in C or G. C's moral text describes the gate (C-026a, running onto pdf 27: the peacock making its wheel; the swan with one foot on a death's head and a trumpet), and M's moral text repeats that description. The Declaración adds a second reading: Juno's chariot, meaning greatness; the swan, which announces its own end; and the Delphic maxim. "Conócete a ti mismo" corresponds to the Latin "Noscete ipsum" engraved at the swan's trumpet on the Naples board (fixed c-swan: "Know thyself."). "para desengaño del que, en hacerla, algunos reciben": "del [engaño] que" is understood, i.e. the self-deception some fall into when they make the wheel. The last clause has no main verb ("con que el tan celebrado dicho … avisando …"); "[comes]" is supplied. "palabras caídas del cielo": translator's note [outside knowledge]: this echoes the Latin commonplace that "Know thyself" came down from heaven (Juvenal, *Satires* 11.27, "e caelo descendit γνῶθι σεαυτόν").
:::

::: seg id=M-42v-a fol=42v–43r kind=rules replaces=C-066b squares=63
ES: En la postrera casa está una palma, premio del trabajo que en ello se pone, y asido a ella está un hombre que ha perdido un zapato por pescar un pez, mostrando que no hay gloria sin dificultad se alcance [43r].
EN: In the last house there is a palm, the prize for the labor that is put into it; and clinging to it is a man who has lost a shoe for catching a fish, showing that there is no glory that is won without difficulty.
NOTE: Not in C or G at this point. C's moral text describes the palm and the man (C-053b, C-055b), and so does M's. On the board, 63 is the VITORIA aedicule with the palm; the man with the fish and the lost shoe stands beside it on the shore of the central sea. "sin dificultad se alcance": the relative "que" is omitted ("no glory [that] is won without difficulty"). "por pescar un pez": "for catching a fish", i.e. at the price of a shoe.
:::

::: seg id=M-43r-a fol=43r kind=rules replaces=C-066b squares=none
ES: En las otras casas hay ciertas figuras y correspondencias, que por sus significados puede rastrear el que con atención lo mirare qué es a lo que se aventura y qué medios le son necesarios. Y como cuando haya hecho todo lo que ha sido de su parte, serán dudosos sus fines, para que de tal manera se gobierne y lo trate que no le cause daño juzgar por suyo lo que pende de voluntad de otro.
EN: In the other houses there are certain figures and correspondences, from whose meanings anyone who looks at them attentively can trace what it is he is venturing on and what means he needs; and how, when he has done everything that was in his power, his ends will still be doubtful. [They are there] so that he may govern himself and handle the matter in such a way that it does him no harm to have counted as his own what hangs on another's will.
NOTE: Not in C or G. The second sentence is syntactically loose ("Y como cuando haya hecho … serán dudosos sus fines, para que …"); it is read as a continuation of what the attentive viewer "can trace", followed by a purpose clause. "correspondencias": the fit between each picture and its meaning. The last clause restates the verse of the Pass of Hope (h15: "There is no hope worth having still / that rests upon another's will.").
:::

::: seg id=M-43r-b fol=43r–43v kind=rules replaces=C-066b squares=none
ES: Y con esto podrá con más facilidad [43v] y menos dolor prevenir al remedio en lo que es veras y advertir en lo que es juego lo siguiente.
EN: And with this he will be able, with more ease and less pain, to provide in advance for the remedy in what is earnest, and to take note, in what is play, of the following.
NOTE: Not in C or G. *veras / juego* = earnest / play: the pair of the prologue ("mostrar entre burla y juego las veras y desventuras"). "prevenir al remedio" = to forestall, provide the remedy beforehand. "lo siguiente" introduces the rules proper.
:::

::: seg id=M-43v-a fol=43v–44r kind=rules replaces=C-066b squares=none
ES: Estando este papel tendido sobre una tabla o pegado en ella, y habiendo puesto cada uno de los que quisieren jugar un tanto del valor que se concertaren, se juega con dos dados pintados por todas partes, desde uno hasta seis puntos, o con seis arenillas pintadas por una. Y pueden jugar en él dos o más personas, los que quisieren [44r], que cuantos más fueren tiene más donaire el juego, por el derribarse de los competidores unos a otros, y el bajar de los que iban confiados muy adelante el subir, y llevarse la polla el que menos pensaba.
EN: With this sheet spread out on a board, or pasted onto one, and each of those who wish to play having put in a stake of whatever value they agree, it is played with two dice marked on every side from one to six points, or with six arenillas marked on one side. Two or more people can play on it, as many as wish; for the more there are, the more fun the game is, with the competitors knocking one another down, those who were climbing confidently far ahead coming down, and the pot being carried off by the one who least expected it.
NOTE: Not in C or G, neither of which says how many play, what is staked, or what the dice are. G's and C's rules speak of "el dado" (the die) in the singular [@sanchezEdicionesAntiguasFilosofia2016oct16, 189]; M itself later says "con el dado" (M-44r-a) and "con los puntos del dado" (M-48r-b). Two dice are needed for the throws of 9 and 10 in M's example and for the "suerte de once" (a throw of 11) that M places on square 53. *arenillas*: dice marked with points on one face only (RAE, *Diccionario de la lengua española*, s.v. "arenilla", obsolete sense<!-- as recorded in notes/web_context.md -->); the counting rule for six arenillas is not stated. Left untranslated with a gloss. "tabla" = a board or table-top. "un tanto" = a stake (one counter of the agreed value); "la polla" = the pot. "el bajar de los que iban confiados muy adelante el subir" is compressed; read as "the coming down of those who were going far ahead, confident in their climb". The transcription's "tiene más donaire el juego" = the game has more grace or wit (more fun).
:::

::: seg id=M-44r-a fol=44r–44v kind=rules replaces=C-066b squares=none
ES: Ha de llevar cada uno de los jugadores una señal para ponerla en la casa y el número de los puntos que pintare con el dado, el que le lanzare jugando por su orden y pasándola a la casa y número de los puntos que [44v] más echare cuando tornare a jugar.
EN: Each of the players must have a marker, to put it on the house whose number is that of the points he throws with the die (whoever throws it, playing in his turn), and then to move it on to the house and number reached by the further points he throws when he plays again.
NOTE: C's single sentence "EL Que lança el dado, se señala a tantas casas, como puntos echo" (C-066b; G "El que lanza el dado se señala a tantas casas como puntos echó" [@sanchezEdicionesAntiguasFilosofia2016oct16, 189]) is the condensed counterpart of this rule. The Spanish is tangled ("para ponerla en la casa y el número de los puntos que pintare con el dado, el que le lanzare jugando por su orden y pasándola…"); the translation keeps its order. "pintare" = "shows" (literally "paints"). "por su orden" = in turn; M does not say who starts beyond the example ("jugó Pedro de mano").
:::

::: seg id=M-44v-a fol=44v–45r kind=rules replaces=C-066b squares=4,6,8
ES: Ejemplo: juegan tres: Pedro, Diego, Rodrigo. Tomó Pedro por señal una sortija, y Diego un real de a dos, y Rodrigo un doblón, y si hubiere más jugadores, ha de tener cada uno su señal diferente uno de otro. Con esto jugó Pedro de mano y echó 6 puntos: puso por señal su sortija en la casa 6. Jugó luego Diego y echó 4 puntos, con que llegó a la casa del trabajo. Y porque, como dice [45r] su moralidad, fol. 14, no debe el que pretende reparar en ningún trabajo, a trueque de alcanzar lo que desea, ha de pasar otros tantos puntos adelante y poner su señal, que es un real de a dos, en la casa 8. Y esta regla se ha de guardar todas cuantas veces se diere en trabajo, que es en cualquier casa donde hubiere bueyes.
EN: An example. Three are playing: Pedro, Diego and Rodrigo. Pedro took a ring as his marker, Diego a two-real piece and Rodrigo a doubloon; and if there are more players, each must have his own marker, different from the others'. With this Pedro played first and threw 6 points: he put his ring as marker on house 6. Then Diego played and threw 4 points, with which he reached the house of Labor. And because, as its moral reading says (fol. 14), whoever petitions must not balk at any labor in exchange for gaining what he desires, he must pass on as many points again and put his marker, which is a two-real piece, on house 8. And this rule must be kept every time one lands on Labor, that is, on any house where there are oxen.
NOTE: Not in C or G. Lucero quotes this passage [@sanchezEdicionesAntiguasFilosofia2016oct16, 190] without the cross-reference "fol. 14", which in the transcription points to M's own foliation (the Labor moralization on fols. 14r–16r). The sources we cite do not say whether the reference is Madrigal's. "jugó Pedro de mano" = Pedro had the lead (*mano* as in card play: the first to play). The markers: a ring; a *real de a dos* (a silver coin of two reales); a *doblón* (a gold doubloon, worth two escudos). On the Naples board square 4 is a Labor square (two yoked oxen under a festoon of fruit) and 6 and 8 are plain; Barros's arithmetic fits that layout. "moralidad" = the moral reading (the explanatory prose).
:::

::: seg id=M-45r-a fol=45r kind=rules replaces=C-066b squares=5,15,26
ES: Jugó luego Rodrigo y echó 5 puntos; puso su doblón en la casa 5. Tornó a jugar Pedro, y sobre 6 que tenía echó 9 [45r], con que llegó a la casa 15 que es el paso de esperanza; y mirando a lo que está escrito encima, cerrado con una raya, halla que dice “Al privado. un. 26. pi.”, que quiere decir que no ha de parar allí, sino ir a buscar favor a la casa del privado. Paga un tanto a la polla por la buena esperanza que se le ofrece y, poniendo su señal en la casa 26, paga otro por el favor que espera.
EN: Then Rodrigo played and threw 5 points; he put his doubloon on house 5. Pedro played again and, on top of the 6 he had, threw 9, with which he reached house 15, which is the Pass of Hope. Looking at what is written above it, enclosed by a line, he finds that it says "To the Favorite, no. 26, pay", which means that he is not to stop there but to go and seek favor at the house of the Favorite. He pays one stake into the pot for the good hope held out to him, and, putting his marker on house 26, pays another for the favor he hopes for.
NOTE: Not in C or G. The label: the transcription reads "Al privado. un. 26. pi.", which looks like a misreading of an abbreviated "nº. 26. pa[ga]" (the Naples board's label beside square 15 reads "Al priuato ·nº· 26. Paga", in a ruled box: "cerrado con una raya"). The evident sense is translated; the M print itself was not available to us. Our observation: Barros quotes the label in Spanish ("Al privado"), while Cartaro's is Italian ("Al priuato"), so the lost Madrid board presumably carried Spanish labels, as Lucero supposes on other grounds [@luceroTableroJuego15882019, 203]. Payment: one stake per square, two in all (so also the general rule, M-47r-a; C "pagando vn tanto por cada vna").

::: seg id=M-46r-a fol=46r kind=rules replaces=C-066b squares=5,15,26
ES: Juega luego Rodrigo, que estaba en 5, y echó 10. Llegó con ellos al paso de esperanza; ha de pasar, mirando lo que está escrito encima, a la casa del privado, hállala ocupada con la sortija de Pedro, quítala, y pone su doblón. Paga dos tantos por la razón arriba dicha, y vuelve Pedro su señal a la casa 5, que es donde estaba Rodrigo, pagando un tanto porque volvió atrás.
EN: Then Rodrigo, who was on 5, plays and throws 10. With them he reached the Pass of Hope. Looking at what is written above it, he must pass on to the house of the Favorite; he finds it occupied by Pedro's ring, removes it and puts down his doubloon. He pays two stakes for the reason given above, and Pedro moves his marker back to house 5, which is where Rodrigo was, paying one stake because he went back.
NOTE: Not in C or G. This is the only worked case of the displacement rule ("se ha de quedar el segundo, y el primero ha de tomar la que el otro dejó", M-46r-b; C-066b "se queda el segundo, y el primero toma la que el otro dexo") and of the payment for going back ("pagando un tanto por cada vez que volviere atrás", M-48r-a; C-066b). It shows that the backward-move payment applies to a player who is displaced, not only to one who overshoots the palm.
:::

::: seg id=M-46r-b fol=46r–47r kind=rules replaces=C-066b squares=none
ES: Y así por esta orden se ha de ir jugando, y advirtiendo que, aunque [46v] se ha hecho relación de solas estas casas y de tres jugadores, se ha de entender que es lo mismo de las otras casas y de los jugadores que más hubiere, de manera que si dieren dos en una casa, se ha de quedar el segundo, y el primero ha de tomar la que el otro dejó. Y si fuere en el principio del juego, se ha de quedar sin casa el primero, pues el que después jugó no la tenía. Y si da en los bueyes, pasa otras tantas casas delante como puntos [47r] echó para llegar allí.
EN: And so, in this order, play must go on. Bear in mind that, although an account has been given only of these houses and of three players, the same is to be understood of the other houses and of however many more players there are. So if two land on one house, the second must stay and the first must take the one the other left. And if this happens at the beginning of the game, the first is left without a house, since the one who played after him had none. And if one lands on the oxen, he passes on as many houses again as the points he threw to get there.
NOTE: From "de manera que si dieren dos en una casa" the text runs parallel to C-066b (pdf 66: "y si dos dan en vna casa, se queda el segundo, y el primero toma la que el otro dexo: y si da en los bueyes, passa otras tantas casas adelante, como pu[n]tos echo, para llegar alli") and to G ("si dos dan en una casa, se queda el segundo, y el primero toma la que el otro dejó" [@sanchezEdicionesAntiguasFilosofia2016oct16, 189]). The sentence "Y si fuere en el principio del juego, se ha de quedar sin casa el primero, pues el que después jugó no la tenía" is absent from C (Lucero's quotation of G breaks off after "el otro dejó;" and resumes at Poverty, so whether G had it is not shown): the displaced player at the start has no square to go to and is off the board. "delante" (corpus) = "adelante".
:::

EN: Having reached the Pass of Hope, which is at fifteen houses, he passes on to that of the Favorite, which is at twenty-six, paying one stake for each of the two. "habiendo estado sin jugar una mano": literally "having been without playing one round"; the order of paying and missing the round is not specified. "vuelve a la suerte cincuenta y tres": *suerte* = the Dice square (a "throw"), fixed "the Dice" (pun: *suerte* = throw / lot). Square names as fixed in the guide.

## Not covered here

- M passages that agree with C in sense (the moral text of every square, the sonnets, prologue, dedication, the Fortune description apart from the three additions): translated with C by the other translators.
- M's two woodcuts (Vázquez's arms on the title page; Fortune on fol. 36r) are images, not text; they are mentioned in M-01r-a and M-37r-a.
