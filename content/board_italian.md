# The Naples board (Cartaro, 1588): every inscription, with literal English

**Source.** Mario Cartaro's engraved board, Naples 1588 (British Museum 1869,0410.2463.+), as transcribed in the board files<!-- `content/squares_01_31.json`, `content/margins_01_31.json`, `content/squares_32_63.json` and `content/centre.json` (readings discussed in `notes/board_squares_01_31.md`, `notes/board_squares_32_63.md`) -->. The Italian is copied from those files, which reproduce the engraving diplomatically: u/v, accents and apostrophes as cut, abbreviations expanded in [ ], long s as s. The glosses are **literal**; the verse translations for players are the fixed ones of the translation guide<!-- `content/TRANSLATION_GUIDE.md` -->, which render the Spanish. "Note" says how the Italian differs **in sense** from the Spanish engraved beside it (and, where it matters, from Barros's book). Spelling differences are ignored.

**Layout, briefly.** Each emblem square has an Italian heading, an Italian couplet above a rule and the Spanish couplet below. Rule labels (instructions) sit in separate boxes on the track's inner edge. All rule labels are in Italian only, except the bilingual one at 32 and the motto on the palm at 63, Italian above Spanish. The four corner mottoes are in Spanish or Latin only, the gate motto in Italian only; the title is Spanish, and the Naples privilege Italian [@luceroTableroJuego15882019, 203 n. 14]. Blank squares (number and plants only) have no inscription: 2, 3, 5, 6, 8, 9, 11, 13, 14, 16, 18, 19, 21, 22, 24, 25, 27, 29, 31, 33, 35, 37, 38, 40, 42, 44, 45, 47, 49, 50, 52, 54, 56, 58, 59, 61, 62.

## Squares 1–31 (outer ring)

| Sq. | Element | Italian as engraved | Literal English | How the Italian differs from the Spanish |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | verse | Guarda ai piedi la raggione / e alla ruota l'openione | Reason looks at the feet, / and Opinion at the wheel. | Same sense as the Spanish (s1); "guarda" = "looks", as "mira". (Spanish on the board: "A los pies mira razon / y a la rueda la opinion") |
| 4 | heading | trauaglio | Labor (lit. "travail, toil") | — |
| 4 | verse | Il trauaglio all'hor ti sente / quand'il premio è poco ò nie[n]te | Labor is felt by you [lit. "feels you"] then, / when the reward is little or nothing. | Spanish: "Labor is never felt except when the reward is low." Italian turns "never … except when" into "then … when" and adds "or nothing". "ti sente" (lit. "feels you") is odd; evidently "you feel it". Heading: Italian *travaglio* means toil, trouble, affliction; it lacks the everyday "work" of Spanish *trabajo*, so the work/hardship pun is only half kept. (Spanish on the board: "Nunca se siente el trabajo / sino qua[n]do el premio es bajo") |
| 7 | heading | Il prodigo | The Prodigal | — |
| 7 | verse | Il prodigo ha gli amici allato / mentre mangia accompag[n]ato | The prodigal has friends at his side / while he eats in company. | Spanish "qua[n]do come co[n] testigos" ("when he eats with witnesses"): Italian "accompag[n]ato" ("in company") loses the barb that the friends are only witnesses to the prodigal's spending. The board's Spanish has "quando" (when) where the book has "Quanto" (as long as). (Spanish on the board: "El prodigo tiene amigos / qua[n]do come co[n] testigos") |
| 10 | heading | Adulatione | Flattery | — |
| 10 | verse | L'adulatione e inganno / uendon la mostra bona è tristo pa[n]no | Flattery and deceit / sell the good sample and the sorry cloth. | Same trade image (the fine sample shown, the bad cloth delivered). Spanish "fina / falso" ("fine / false") becomes "bona / tristo" ("good / wretched"). The board's Spanish reverses the book's line order ("Vende adulacion y e[n]ganno / muestra fina…"); the Italian follows the board's order. (Spanish on the board: "Vende adulacion y e[n]ganno / muestra fina y falso paño") |
| 12 | heading | trauaglio | Labor | — |
| 12 | verse | Frutti del trauaglio giusto / sono honor utile, e gusto | Fruits of just labor / are honor, profit and pleasure. | Same; "utile" = profit, use. (Spanish on the board: "Frutos del trabajo justo / son honrra prouecho y gusto") |
| 15 | heading | Passo della speranza | Pass of Hope | — |
| 15 | verse | Nulla spera[n]za per buona s'intende / che dalla uolonta d'altrui dipe[n]de | No hope is reckoned good / that depends on the will of others. | Same sense. Spanish "que esta en uoluntad agena" ("that lies in another's will"); Italian adds "s'intende" ("is understood, reckoned") and "dipe[n]de" ("depends"). (Spanish on the board: "Ninguna esperança es buena / que esta en uoluntad agena") |
| 15 | rule label | Al priuato ·n[umer]º· 26. Paga | To the Favorite, no. 26. Pay. | Italian only ("priuato"; Spanish would be "priuado"). Barros's Madrigal text quotes this label in Spanish, "Al privado … 26" (Madrid passage M-45r-a), so the lost Madrid board presumably had it in Spanish (our inference). |
| 17 | heading | trauaglio | Labor | — |
| 17 | verse | Vien dal'otio pouertade / dal trauaglio facoltade | From idleness comes poverty, / from labor, means. | Same; "facoltade" = means, substance, for Spanish "riqueza" ("wealth"). (Spanish on the board: "Del ocio nace pobreza / y del trabajo riqueza") |
| 20 | heading | Diligentia | Diligence | — |
| 20 | verse | Quanto il mondo si trauaglia / cosa uana e come paglia | However much the world toils, / it is a vain thing, and like straw. | Spanish: "All that the world labors and strives for is rubbish" (*basura*: refuse, dung, matching the dung-beetle's ball on the square). Italian "paglia" ("straw") loses the dung and the link to the picture, adds "uana" ("vain"), and drops "procura" ("strives"). (Spanish on the board: "Quanto trabaja y procura / el mundo todo es basura") |
| 23 | heading | tauaglio [sic] | Labor (engraved "tauaglio", sic, without the r) | — |
| 23 | verse | Non si chiama quel trauaglio / do[n]de uscirne posso et uaalio | That is not called hardship / from which I can get out, and have the strength to. | Spanish: "That is no great hardship which [work] suffices to get us out of." Italian drops "great" and shifts to the first person: "I" can, and have the strength ("posso et uaalio", i.e. "vaglio", I avail, I am equal to it), to get out, where in Spanish it is labor that frees "us". (Spanish on the board: "No es grande trabajo aq[ue]l / que basta a sacarnos del") |
| 26 | heading | Il priuato | The Favorite | Engraved "priuato", with the u cut like an n (three minims after "pri"), as in the label at 15, "Al priuato". |
| 26 | verse | Non cercar mano aliena / se la tua non serà piena | Do not seek another's hand / if yours will not be full. | Same sense; "cercar" ("seek") for "pidas" ("ask"); future "serà" ("will be") for "va" ("goes"). (Spanish on the board: "No pidas la mano agena / si la tuya no va llena") |
| 26 | rule label | Paga. | Pay. | The same word in both languages. Barros: one stake here, one at 15. |
| 28 | heading | Sorte | The Throw (*sorte*: lot, chance, a throw) | — |
| 28 | verse | A'cui fortuna è auara / la sorte diuien zara | For him to whom Fortune is miserly, / the throw turns into "zara". | Spanish: "If there is no luck in pressing one's case (*negociar*), the throw turns to *azar*." Italian replaces the pressing of cases at court with a stingy Fortune. The dice pun survives: *zara* is the old Italian dice game, and a losing throw, a word of the same Arabic origin as Spanish *azar*. Board Spanish "acar" (no cedilla). (Spanish on the board: "Si no ay dicha en negociar / la suerte buelue acar") |
| 30 | heading | trauaglio | Labor | — |
| 30 | verse | S'arrende al fin fortuna / s'il trauaglio l'inportuna | Fortune surrenders in the end / if labor importunes her. | Same. (Spanish on the board: "Al fin se rinde fortuna / si el trabajo la importuna") |

## Squares 32–63 (end of outer ring, inner ring, Victory)

| Sq. | Element | Italian as engraved | Literal English | How the Italian differs from the Spanish |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 32 | heading | Pozzo del oblio | Well of Oblivion | — |
| 32 | verse | Pone in oblio l'ingrato / tutto il ben che li fù dato | The ingrate puts into oblivion / all the good that was given him. | Same sense; "che li fù dato" ("that was given him") for "hae recibido" ("he has received"). The board's Spanish ("Pone el ingrato en oluido / quanto bien hae recibido") differs in wording, not sense, from the book's ("El ingrato echa en oluido / Quanto bien ha recebido"). (Spanish on the board: "Pone el ingrato en oluido / quanto bien hae recibido") |
| 32 | rule label | Per le fune ·1· et ·2· fermesi / Para·sogas ·1· y ·2· estese | For the ropes, 1 and 2; let him stop. / For ropes, 1 and 2; let him stay. | The only bilingual rule label: Italian above, Spanish below; "fermesi" = "si fermi", "estese" = "estése" (let him stay). It abbreviates Barros's rule: one stake to each player and two to the pot "for ropes", and miss one round. |
| 34 | heading | trauaglio | Labor | — |
| 34 | verse | Il frutto della spene / con il trauaglio uiene | The fruit of hope / comes with labor. | Same; "spene" is the poetic word for hope; "uiene" ("comes") for "se alcança" ("is reached"). (Spanish on the board: "El fruto dela esperança / por el trabajo se alcança") |
| 36 | heading | Che diranno | What will they say | — |
| 36 | verse | Chi seru' à quel che diranno / pigli il soldo che li danno | Who serves "what they will say", / let him take the wage they give him. | Same sense; "soldo" (a wage, a soldier's pay, a coin) for "pago" ("payment"). Board Spanish "sireue" (sic). (Spanish on the board: "El que sireue al que diran / tome el pago q[ue] le dan") |
| 36 | rule label | Alla sorte ·n·° 28 ·Paga | To the Throw, no. 28. Pay. | Italian only. "la sorte" here = the Dice square at 28. |
| 39 | heading | Falsa amicitia | False friendship | — |
| 39 | verse | Rendon gratie per aggrauij / quando negotian gli sauij | The wise give thanks for injuries / when they press their cases. | Spanish: "By giving thanks for injuries, wise men press their cases": the thanks are the means. Italian makes them the occasion ("quando", "when"). Board Spanish "Dondo" (sic for "Dando"). (Spanish on the board: "Dondo gra[cia]s por agrauios / negocian los honbres sabios") |
| 39 | rule label | Al prodigo ·n·° 7 ·Paga | To the Prodigal, no. 7. Pay. | Italian only. |
| 41 | heading | trauaglio | Labor | — |
| 41 | verse | Trauaglio e il non hauere / da poter mangiare e bere | Hardship is not having [the means] / to be able to eat and drink. | Spanish: "It is hardship not to have it [work], for one who must eat by it", a pun on *trabajo* (work / hardship). Italian loses both the pun and the work: hardship is simply lacking food and drink. (Spanish on the board: "Trabajo es no le tener / el q[ue] del a de comer") |
| 43 | heading | Mutanza d'ministri | Change of ministers | — |
| 43 | verse | Chi limita sua speranza / soffra il colpo de mutanza | Who limits his hope, / let him suffer the blow of change. | Same. Board Spanish "Quin" (sic for "Quien"). (Spanish on the board: "Quin limita su esperança / sufra el golpe de mudança") |
| 43 | rule label | Alla adulatione·n·° 10 ·Paga | To Flattery, no. 10. Pay. | Italian only. |
| 46 | heading | Morte de lo ualitore | Death of the patron (*valitore*, a Hispanism from *valedor*, not standard Italian) | — |
| 46 | verse | L'huom ch'in huomo si confida / resta cieco senza guida | The man who trusts in man / remains blind without a guide. | Spanish "queda qual ciego sin guia" ("is left like a blind man without a guide"): Italian drops the simile (he *is* blind) and has "man" for "men". (Spanish on the board: "El hombre que en ho[m]bres fia / queda qual ciego sin guia") |
| 46 | rule label | In camincia de nuouo ·Paga· | He begins anew. Pay. | Italian only; "In camincia" as engraved, for "Incomincia" (he begins). Barros: back to the start. |
| 48 | heading | trauaglio | Labor | — |
| 48 | verse | Ben che mobil sia fortuna / al trauaglio è oportuna | Although Fortune be changeable, / to labor she is opportune. | Same; "oportuna" ("timely, helpful") for "fauorable". (Spanish on the board: "Aunque fortuna es mudable / al trabajo es fauorable") |
| 51 | heading | Casa della fortuna | House of Fortune | — |
| 51 | verse | Tutto :sta à dispositione / di fortuna et permissione | All stands at the disposition / of Fortune and permission. | Same. The colon-like mark in "Tutto :sta" / "Todo :esta" is engraved in both languages. (Spanish on the board: "Todo :esta a dispusicion / de fortuna y permision") |
| 51 | rule label | Giuoca ·2· uolte · | Plays twice. | Italian only; "Giuoca" = he plays (or "play!"). Barros: throw twice. |
| 53 | heading | Sorte | The Throw | — |
| 53 | verse | Ogni sorte in mal fin cade / oue alberga pouertade | Every throw comes to a bad end / where poverty lodges. | Spanish "Qual quier suerte es de tristeza" ("any throw is one of sadness") becomes "in mal fin cade" ("falls to a bad end"), and "albergare" ("to lodge") makes Poverty a lodger in the house. The board's Spanish has the lines in the order of the princeps G, not of the M and C books (first-edition reading G-35); Rodríguez Mansilla notes that the board reverses the lines of Madrigal's text [@rodriguezmansillaGRABADOPICARAJUSTINA2019, 190]. (Spanish on the board: "Qual quier suerte es de tristeza / en la casa do ay pobreza") |
| 55 | heading | Pensar che | To think that (infinitive; Spanish *Pensé que* = "I thought that") | — |
| 55 | verse | Dal pensai che, la uentura / fugge et quella che tien poco li dura | From "I thought that", good fortune / flees, and what he has lasts him little. | Same sense; "poco li dura" ("lasts him little") for "no dura" ("does not last"). The heading uses the infinitive "Pensar che" ("thinking that"), while the verse has "pensai che" ("I thought that"); only the Spanish keeps a single fixed phrase. (Spanish on the board: "Del pense que huye uentura / y la que tiene no dura") |
| 55 | rule label | Alla diligentia n·° 20 ·Paga | To Diligence, no. 20. Pay. | Italian only. |
| 57 | heading | trauaglio | Labor | — |
| 57 | verse | Il trauaglio apparta palma / et fa relucer l'alma | Labor brings [engraved "apparta", sets apart] the palm / and makes the soul shine. | Spanish: "Labor wins the palm and removes the rust from the soul." "apparta" is so engraved; as written it would mean "sets apart"; read as "apporta", it means "brings". Either way it is weaker than "gana" ("wins"). The scouring of rust becomes making the soul shine ("fa relucer"): same idea, but the rust image is lost. (Spanish on the board: "El trabajo gana palma / y quita el orin del alma") |
| 60 | heading | Casa d'la pouerta | House of Poverty (engraved "pouerta", no accent) | — |
| 60 | verse | Pouertà secca l'humore / alla radice del fauore | Poverty dries the sap / at the root of favor. | Same. (Spanish on the board: "Pobreza seca el humor / dela raiz del fauor") |
| 60 | rule label | Limosina et alla sorte·53 | Alms, and to the Throw, 53. | Italian only; "Limosina" as engraved. There is no "Paga": the player receives alms (Barros: one stake from each player, in M). |
| 63 | heading | VITORIA | VICTORY (engraved "VITORIA", the Spanish spelling; Italian would be *Vittoria*) | — |
| 63 | verse | Quando ch'hai maggior fortuna / pensa che mobil e più che la luna | When you have greater fortune, / think that it is more changeable than the moon. | Spanish: "When you have most fortune, see that it is like the moon." Italian sharpens the simile into a comparison ("more changeable than the moon") and has "pensa" ("think") for "mira" ("see, consider"). (Spanish on the board: "Quando tengas mas fortuna / mira que es como la luna") |
| 63 | rule label | Ne il poco ne lassai · / Ni lo mucho·ni la poco | Neither the little nor the much. / Neither the much nor the little. | The exact-throw motto on the palm trunk (Barros: neither more nor fewer points than needed). The Italian ("Ne il poco ne lassai", i.e. "ne l'assai") reverses the Spanish order, little before much. Board Spanish "la poco" (sic). |

## Center, palm and Fortune's banderole

| Element | Italian as engraved | Literal English | How the Italian differs from the Spanish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Central cartouche (the sea) | Mare di soffrimento / chi pretende hà dà soffrire / come chi nasce il morire | Sea of suffering / whoever aspires must suffer, / as whoever is born [must suffer] dying. | Same (verse "sea"). Italian *pretendere* = to claim, aspire; it does not carry the technical courtly sense of Spanish *pretender*, "to petition for office". *soffrimento* = suffering; like Spanish *sufrimiento*, it can also mean endurance. Spanish on the board: "Mar de suffrimiento / quien pretende ha de sus: srir / como el que nace morir" ("sussrir", sic). |
| Numerals in the aedicule | 6 3 | 63 | — |
| Cartouche below the palm | VITORIA | Victory | Spanish only (the Spanish spelling; no Italian form given). |
| Verses of the man at the palm | Mai salirà gran costa / che mira quanto costa | He will never climb a great slope / who looks at how much it costs. | Same, and the pun survives: Italian *costa* = hillside, and *costa* = it costs, as Spanish *cuesta* / *cuesta* (verse h63b). Spanish on the board: "Nunca subira gran cuesta / quien mira · lo q[ue] cuesta" ("mira", not the book's "mirare"). |
| Fortune's banderole (square 51) | cambio et / muto il consiglio | I change and alter counsel. | Italian only on the board. Barros's Spanish (book): "Yo trueco, y mudo el co[n]sejo" (h51b, "I exchange and alter counsel"); same sense. |

## Corners, gate, title, privileges, signature

| Element | Text as engraved | Language | Literal English | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Entrance gate | Guarda l'fine | Italian | Look to the end. (lit. "Watch the end") | Italian only; no Spanish on the board (fixed c-gate). Barros's Spanish has the trumpet warn each person to "mire al fin de lo que pretende" ("look to the end of what he seeks"). |
| Corner, bottom left (swan with trumpet on a skull) | Noscete ipsum | Latin | Know thyself. | Latin only ("Nosce te ipsum", engraved as one word; fixed c-swan). Barros's Declaración (M only) gives it in Spanish, "Conócete a ti mismo". |
| Corner, top left (dolphin and anchor) | Dateprisa aespacio | Spanish | Make haste slowly. (lit. "Hurry yourself at leisure") | Spanish only; engraved "a espacio", not "despacio" (fixed c-dolphin). Barros: speed and firmness. |
| Corner, top right (Occasion reclining) | No me pierdas | Spanish | Don't lose me. | Spanish only (fixed c-occasion). |
| Corner, bottom right (hand and clock) | Haesta la postrera | Spanish | Until the last [hour]. | Spanish only; engraved "Haesta" (for "Hasta"; possibly "Ha esta"). Barros: "hasta la postrera hora" (fixed c-clock). |
| Title, top margin | FILOSOFIA CORTESANA … DE ALONSO DE BARROS | Spanish | The Courtly Philosophy … of Alonso de Barros | Spanish only; Filosofía cortesana kept in Spanish in running text. |
| Title, completion, bottom right | CRIADO DEL REY N[UEST]RO S[EÑ]OR | Spanish | servant of the King our lord | Completes the title: "… de Alonso de Barros, criado del Rey nuestro Señor". |
| Under the title | Con su priuilegio | Spanish | With his [or: its] privilege. | "su" = his (the king's, or Barros's) or its (the work's): the Castilian royal privilege of 9 February 1587, as in the book. |
| Naples privilege, bottom margin | Con priuilegio di Sua Ecc[ellenz]a per ·X· / anni nel Regno di Napoli | Italian | With the privilege of His Excellency for ten years in the Kingdom of Naples. | Italian only. "Sua Ecc[ellenz]a" = the viceroy of Naples; "X." = ten (Roman numeral). Lucero: "privilegio de diez años para su impresión en el reino de Nápoles" [@luceroTableroJuego15882019, 202]. |
| Engraver's signature, bottom right | Marius·Cartarius.Jnc[idit] Neap[oli]·1588· | Latin | Mario Cartaro engraved [it], Naples, 1588. | Latin; "Cartarius" (Mario Cartaro); "Jnc[idit]" = engraved. |
