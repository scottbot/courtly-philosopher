# Translation guide — Barros, *Filosofía cortesana* (English web edition)

## Voice
- Faithful first, then engaging. A lay English reader should enjoy it; a Hispanist should
  find nothing added, nothing dropped, and every figure of speech kept.
- Modern, clear, slightly elevated English. No mock-Elizabethan ("thee", "doth"), no
  modern slang.
- Spelling: US, in the edition's own English (the editor's decision): "honor", "labor", "favor",
  "color", "center", "-ize", "traveled", "toward", "plow", "meager". Never change spelling inside
  a quotation from another author, or in the Spanish. (The fixed verses below still have British
  forms; they are converted in the final spelling pass.)
- Barros writes long periods strung with "y", "que", colons. Keep his order of ideas;
  you MAY split a period into two or three English sentences, never merge paragraphs,
  never reorder ideas, never summarize.
- Keep his puns and double senses and flag them in a translator's note when English
  loses them (e.g. *suerte* = both "a throw of the dice" and "lot/luck"; *trabajo* =
  "work" and "hardship"; *cuesta* = "slope" and "it costs"; *casa* = "house" of the board
  and "household"; *azar* = the losing throw at dice and "misfortune").
- Proper nouns of squares: capitalize as in the list below.
- Square brackets in the Spanish (editorial expansions) are silently used; [sic] misprints
  are translated as the evident sense, with a note if the sense is doubtful. `[?]`
  readings: translate the likely sense and add a note.
- Damaged type in the Spanish transcription: where a letter is broken, partly uninked or
  cut like another but its identity is evident (*ttabajo*, *ha le*, *Mi:a*), ES prints the
  evident letters, with no mark, and the segment NOTE records the damaged form as printed.
  `[sic]` is kept for misprints (a wrong word or letter set by the compositor, e.g. *riende*,
  *fusse*), and `[?]` for readings that are genuinely doubtful (e.g. *huy[?]*, letters obscured
  by show-through). A tilde over a vowel is expanded as the nasal only: *pretẽsor* =
  *prete[n]sor*, *mãda* = *ma[n]da*.
- Outside the translation itself (notes, annotations, essays, interface), never assume the
  gender of the reader, the player or any generic person: reword, or use singular "they"
  or "you"; never "he or she". Named people, Barros's worked-example players and figures
  drawn as men keep their pronouns.

## The verses (letras): FIXED translations — use exactly these
Each has a verse rendering (shown to players) and a literal rendering (shown beneath).

| id | Spanish (Naples 1588 book) | verse | literal |
|---|---|---|---|
| adam | No puede el hijo de Adam / Sin trabajo comer pan. | No son of Adam can be fed / unless by labor for his bread. | The son of Adam cannot eat bread without labor. |
| s1 | A los pies mira razon, / Y a la rueda la opinion. | Reason looks down to the feet; / Opinion, to the wheel's conceit. | Reason looks at the feet, and Opinion at the wheel. |
| t0 | Nunca se siente el trabajo, / Sino quando el premio es baxo. | Labor's never felt at all / except when the reward is small. | Labor is never felt except when the reward is low. |
| t1 | Frutos del trabajo justo: / Son onrra; prouecho, y gusto. | The fruits of honest toil are these: / honor, profit, and heart's ease. | The fruits of just labor are honor, profit and pleasure. |
| t2 | Del ocio nazce pobreza: / Y del trabajo riqueza. | From idleness springs poverty, / and out of labor, prosperity. | From idleness is born poverty, and from labor wealth. |
| t3 | No es grande trabajo aquel; / Que basta a sacarnos del. | No trouble's great, if work can be / enough to set us from it free. | That is no great hardship which suffices to get us out of it. |
| t4 | Al fin se riende fortuna; / Si el trabajo la importuna. | Fortune at last will yield the day / if labor will not go away. | In the end Fortune gives in, if labor pesters her. |
| t5 | El fruto de la esperanca; / Por el trabajo se alcanca. | The fruit to which our hopes aspire / it is by labor we acquire. | The fruit of hope is reached through labor. |
| t6 | Trabajo no le tener; / El que del ha de comer. | To have no work is work indeed / for one who works to meet his need. | It is hardship not to have it [work], for one who must eat by it. |
| t7 | Aunque fortuna es mudable; / Al trabajo es fauorable. | Though Fortune's ever changeable, / to labor she is favorable. | Although Fortune is changeable, she is favorable to labor. |
| t8 | El trabajo gana palma; / Y quita el orin del alma. | Labor wins the palm, and then / scours the soul of rust again. | Labor wins the palm and removes the rust from the soul. |
| g1 | No se siente el que es mayor, / donde pone el yugo amor. (1587 princeps only) | However great the yoke may prove, / it is not felt when laid by love. | The greatest [yoke] is not felt where love puts on the yoke. |
| g2 | El yugo, reja y arado / no cansan por el amado. (princeps only) | Yoke and plowshare, plow and all, / for love's sake never tire at all. | Yoke, plowshare and plow do not weary, for the beloved's sake. |
| g3 | No oy [hay] trabajo que no acabe, / al que le tiene por grave. (princeps only) | There is no toil but it will break / the one who deems it hard to take. | There is no labor that will not finish off the one who holds it grievous. |
| g6 | Trabajando halla quietud / quien de fuerza hace virtud. (princeps only) | Who makes a virtue of his need / by working finds his rest indeed. | By working, he finds peace who makes a virtue of necessity. |
| h15 | Ninguna esperanca es buena; / Que esta en voluntad ajena. | There is no hope worth having still / that rests upon another's will. | No hope is good that rests on another's will. |
| h26 | No pidas la mano ajena / Si la tuya no va llena. | Before you ask another's hand, / fill yours, or empty-handed stand. | Do not ask for another's hand if yours does not go full. |
| h32 | El ingrato echa en oluido / Quanto bien ha recebido. | The ingrate casts, without a thought, / into oblivion all he got. | The ingrate casts into oblivion all the good he has received. |
| h36 | El que sirue al que diran, / Tome el pago que le dan. | Who serves "What Will They Say?" / must take whatever they pay. | He who serves "what will they say" must take the pay they give him. |
| h28 | Si no ay dicha en negociar, / La suerte se buelue azar. | If luck won't help your case along, / your throw of dice will turn out wrong. | If there is no luck in pressing one's case, the throw turns into a losing one. |
| h39 | Dando gracias por agrauios; / Negocian los hombres sabios. | The wise, when wronged, give thanks instead, / and so their cases go ahead. | Giving thanks for injuries, wise men press their cases. |
| h7 | El prodigo tiene amigos. / Quanto come con testigos. | The prodigal has friends, while he / still dines where witnesses can see. | The prodigal has friends as long as he eats with witnesses. |
| h43 | Quien limita su esperança / Sufra el golpe de mudança. | Who narrows all his hope to one / must bear the blow when change has come. | Whoever limits his hope, let him suffer the blow of change. |
| h10 | Muestra fina, y falso panno; / Vende adulacion, y enganno. | Flattery and Fraud will sell / fine samples, and false cloth as well. | Flattery and deceit sell a fine sample and false cloth. |
| h46 | El hombre que en hombres fia, / Queda qual ciego sin guia. | Who trusts in men is left, you'll find, / without a guide, like one gone blind. | The man who trusts in men is left like a blind man without a guide. |
| h51 | Todo esta a dispusicion, / De fortuna, y permission. | All things are at the disposition / of Fortune and of God's permission. | Everything is at the disposal of Fortune and [divine] permission. |
| h51b | (motto) Yo trueco, y mudo el co[n]sejo | "I exchange and alter counsel." | I exchange and alter counsel. |
| h55 | Del penseque huy[?] ventura: / Y la que tiene no dura. | From "I Thought…" good fortune flies, / and what he has soon fades and dies. | From "I thought that" good fortune flees, and what he has does not last. |
| h20 | Quanto trabaja y procura, / El mundo todo es vasura. | For all the world's labor and care, / it's dung and rubbish everywhere. | All that the world labors and strives for is rubbish. |
| h60 | Pobreza seca el vmor / De la rayz del fauor. | Want dries up the sap, and so / favor's root can no more grow. | Poverty dries up the moisture of the root of favor. |
| h53 | En la casa do ay pobreza: / Qualquier suerte es de tristeza. | In a house where poverty lies, / every throw's a throw of sighs. | In the house where there is poverty, any throw is one of sadness. |
| h63m | (on the palm trunk) Ni lo mucho, ni lo poco. | Neither too much nor too little. | Neither the much nor the little. |
| h63 | Quando tengas mas fortuna, / Mira que es como la luna. | When fortune's fullest, bear in mind: / the moon is of the selfsame kind. | When you have the most fortune, see that it is like the moon. |
